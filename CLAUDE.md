# CLAUDE.md

This file provides guidance to Claude Code (claude.ai/code) when working with code in this repository.

## What this is

The current game is **Chronicle**, an AP U.S. History RPG: a browser-based, top-down field/dialogue game. It covers **CED Periods 1–8 of 9** as of Phase 97 — twenty-four cases across eight units, all with real, cited historical content, on eight walkable outdoor field maps plus ten interiors. **Unit 9's content shipped in Phase 100** and is registered for validation but deliberately not playable, so the repository holds nine units of content and the game a student can walk is eight. Unit 1 "The Atlantic World" (case-001–003), Unit 2 "Colonial Crossroads" (case-004–006), Unit 3 "Revolution and Founding" (case-007–009), Unit 4 "Democracy, Markets, and Reform" (case-010–012), Unit 5 "A House Divided" (case-013–015), Unit 6 "A Continent on Paper" (case-016–018), Unit 7 "The Terms of Belonging" (case-019–021), Unit 8 "Grounds for Refusal" (case-022–024). **Units 1–6 reached parity in Phase 87** — Unit 6's map shipped in Phase 85, its two interiors in Phase 86 and its three activities in Phase 87. **Unit 6 is complete as of Phase 88**, when its Meridian reveal shipped — the last thing it was owed. **Unit 7 became playable in Phase 89C**, when its wharf at Ellis Island shipped and the `UNITS` line was added — so the game a student can walk is now Periods 1–7 — its two interiors shipped in Phase 89D, and **its three missions shipped in Phase 89E, which completes it**. **All eight units are at parity**: every one has a walkable map and three playable missions on it — twenty-four missions in all. **Interiors were never part of that parity** — the ten of them belong to Units 4 through 8, two apiece, and Units 1–3 have none; this sentence claimed otherwise from Phase 89E to Phase 118. **Unit 8 became walkable in Phase 97**, its two interiors shipped in Phase 98 and its three missions in Phase 99, which completes it. Four of Unit 7's seven records keep `activityRoute: null` and open in the reader, which is what every non-mission record on the other six maps does — three missions is the slate, and seven activities would be a different design. See `docs/decision-log/0081-three-missions-at-the-immigrant-station.md`. Unit 8 was built in the documented order — content (Phase 95), art (96), map and cast (97), interiors (98), activities (99) — end to end without a variation worth recording, which is the order **Unit 9 is now following**: its content shipped as Phase 100 (`0099`), its art reconnaissance as Phase 101 (`0100`) and its map's ground as Phase 102 (`0101`), resized to `FIELD_GRID`'s 56×36 in Phase 103 (`0102`), and it owes a cast, two interiors and three activities. Two Unit 9 art gaps were registered and deliberately **not** commissioned, because the PixelLab balance is nearly out and that is a spend decision rather than a build step — and **both closed in Phase 153 without spending anything**, modelled in Blender and rendered to pixel art into `derived/campus-archive.png` (`0152`). **The same balance still blocks Unit 9's cast**, and with it the unit's `FIELD_MAPS` entry and its `UNITS` line — there is no 1990s body in 140 character sheets, so the map's ground is committed with nobody on it. See `0101` §7 for why those three are one decision rather than three omissions. See `docs/decision-log/0094-the-word-is-not-on-the-form.md` through `0100`.

**"Republic Builder Engine" is retired as the project's identity, and as of Phase 81 the string is gone from every rendered surface.** The repository was originally framed as a reusable engine of that name; that framing is no longer current. The eventual multi-subject platform this codebase may grow into **does not yet have a final name** — don't invent one or reintroduce "Republic Builder" branding in new code, docs, or copy. `BRAND.engine` no longer exists (removed from the content file, `BrandSchema`, and its ten render sites), the chrome header is now a one-line lockup, and the tab title, `package.json` name and README front matter all say Chronicle. Two survivals are deliberate: the `localStorage` key `republic-builder.chronicle.unit-01.v2`, because renaming it orphans every existing save, and the repository folder name itself. **Don't "finish the job" on either.**

**Removed systems — do not restore:** Founder Paths, professions, Historian Skills, and clothing/wardrobe-slot systems have been removed from the design and must not be reintroduced. The player identity model is intentionally minimal (display name + one of two appearance choices only — see `docs/decision-log/0005-chronicle-identity-and-first-field-entry.md`); pronouns, wardrobes, professions, cosmetics, and inventory remain deliberately out of scope, not merely deferred to a later milestone.

### Product intent

The goal is a Pokémon-inspired APUSH RPG students use across a full school year — approachable controls, a stylized explorable world, NPC dialogue bubbles, independent NPC movement, location-specific music, and badge/collection progression — not a quiz site or worksheet reskin. Gameplay is meant to train authentic AP U.S. History skills (HIPP source analysis, SAQ/LEQ/DBQ argument construction, evidence-based reasoning) using real primary sources and the actual College Board rubrics where applicable, rather than relying on trivia recall. Game progression must never grant unfair advantages on graded assessments — cosmetic rewards, organizers, and alternate routes are fine; pay-to-win or build-to-win mechanics on assessments are not.

Narrative frame: the player is recruited at the Chronicle Institute (present day) because the historical record has become damaged/unstable, becomes a "Chronicler," and uses "Chronotravel" to visit historical settings, gather evidence from people and primary sources, and transmit/preserve records back at the Institute Archive to earn area badges. Historical dialogue may take reasonable dramatic liberties but should stay in the speaking character's voice — avoid fourth-wall commentary, repeated "this is dramatized" disclaimers inside conversations, or modern educational narration coming out of a historical figure's mouth.

## Commands

Run from the repo root (npm scripts shell out to Vite, which is rooted at `apps/web` via `vite.config.js`):

- `npm run dev` — start the Vite dev server (opens the browser automatically)
- `npm run build` — production build (output in `apps/web/dist/`)
- `npm run preview` — preview a production build
- `npm run validate:content` — real, working Zod-based validator (run `npm run docs:stats` for the current group count). Validates every registered unit's `unit-0N-campaign.js` and `quests/unit-0N-quests.js` against schemas in `apps/web/src/content/schemas/`, plus the 9-unit primary source library, and checks global case/source id-uniqueness, per-quest-type id-uniqueness, case-period-matches-unit, and every archive/investigation/reader/alternate challenge cross-reference. **Its per-unit blocks are derived, not hand-written** (Phase 90J) — `apps/web/src/content/unit-registry.js` holds `UNIT_IDS`, and adding a unit costs one line there plus its imports and one line in `local-content-repository.js`'s `UNIT_MODULES` — **plus a Chronotravel plate and a Navigation Table view**, each of which has a test that fails without it. **One block was still hand-written until Phase 100 and it cost a unit a phase of checking**: `checkActivityRoutes()` iterated `["unit01" … "unit07"]`, so Unit 8's three activities were never cross-checked against its routed sources — and the thing it would have caught was real, because `UNIT_MODULES` still carried that unit's `activities: {}` placeholder. See `docs/decision-log/0099-the-validator-stopped-at-unit-seven.md`. See `0094` §5 for the measured cost, and note that a unit with content but no map must not be handed to `build-field-guide.js`, which documents maps. A content export the validator has no schema for is a **hard error**, not a skip. See `docs/content/CONTENT-VALIDATION.md`.
- `npm run lint` — real, working ESLint flat config (`eslint.config.js`)
- `npm run format` / `npm run format:check` — real, working Prettier
- `npm run assets:blender -- <job>` — renders a Blender job (`scripts/blender/jobs/<job>.py`) headless and pixelizes it into a `Chronicle Commissions/` strip, then prints the manifest boxes and writes a scale preview to `reports/blender/<job>/`. **Blender is a build-time art tool, not a dependency** — found at `BLENDER_PATH` or under Program Files, never needed by CI or the build, because the strip is committed. The look is measured off `office/1` (`0152` §4), not chosen; a pack-style object that fills one tile is rendered to a width, furniture to a scale.

A test runner **is** configured: `npm run test` (Vitest, non-watch, CI-compatible) runs tests from `tests/unit/` — see `docs/development/UNIT-TESTING.md`. ESLint and Prettier **are** configured and working — don't claim otherwise.

Type checking is **editor-only and advisory**, not a gate. `jsconfig.json` turns on `checkJs` for `apps/web/src/engine/**` and `apps/web/src/repositories/**` only — everything else, `main.js` included, is unchecked. There **is** a `npm run typecheck` script (`tsc -p jsconfig.json --noEmit`) and TypeScript **is** a devDependency — but neither makes it a gate: `npm run check` does not invoke it, and CI does not either. Run it on demand. The current baseline is **18 errors, all verified false positives** (untyped `supabase-js` embeds inferring `{}`/arrays, `number[]` where a `[x, y]` tuple is wanted, and Safari's `webkitAudioContext`) — so treat a _new_ error as worth reading, but don't "fix" the standing 18 by changing engine code.

Spell checking is configured in `cspell.json`, with word lists in `.cspell/` — `project-words.txt` (domain/engine/tooling vocabulary) and `source-quotations.txt` (period spellings quoted verbatim from primary sources, which must **not** be "corrected"). The repo is calibrated to zero unknown words, so any hit is a real typo. Check with `npx -y cspell "<glob>"`. Note that `19th Centruy European Dock`, `Labratory`, and `Jamestown Capenter` are misspelled _upstream_ — on disk and in PixelLab respectively — so those strings are correct in code and must stay.

## Architecture

### The app is currently one file

The entire running game is implemented in **`apps/web/src/main.js`** (18,026 lines as of Phase 152 / 2026-09-20 — re-check the line count with a quick `wc -l` before citing it if much time has passed, don't trust a stale figure, and use `wc -l` rather than PowerShell's `Measure-Object -Line`, which under-reports this file by ~340 — this file has grown substantially phase over phase and old figures go stale fast). It owns: screen routing/state machine, field and hub movement/collision/NPC patrol logic, dialogue, the activity host screen, the exchange ledger, the Author Mode panel, quest rendering/grading, the join/login/teacher-dashboard/grading/manage-content screens, and all HTML rendering (via template-literal strings, not a framework — there is no React/Vue/etc.). Web Audio (music + SFX) lives in `apps/web/src/engine/audio-engine.js` — **file-backed since Phase 152**, though `assets/audio/` is still empty and the synthesised loops are what actually play, per-scene, until a track is commissioned (`docs/design/AUDIO-PROMPT-BOOK.md`); real-accounts/classroom/submission/grading logic lives in `apps/web/src/repositories/` and `apps/web/src/engine/` (`auth-flows.js`, `evaluator-requests.js`, `evaluator-client.js`) — both imported by `main.js`.

An orphaned second implementation of the onboarding→field→case-player loop (`apps/web/src/features/*`, plus its two supporting dead stores `engine/content/author-content-store.js` / `engine/player/player-profile-store.js`) used to exist alongside `main.js` — six files total, never imported by it, containing two more dead Author Mode implementations on top of `main.js`'s own broken one. It was confirmed zero-risk (per `docs/architecture/ARCHITECTURE-REVIEW-AND-SIMPLIFICATION.md`) and deleted in a dead-code-removal pass — see `docs/migrations/DEAD-CODE-REMOVAL.md`. Don't recreate it; when extending gameplay, edit `main.js` directly unless deliberately doing modularization work.

**All eight authored units** (`content/unit-01-campaign.js` through `unit-08-campaign.js`) carry real, cited historical content across every case, plus per-unit Archive Challenges, and **all eight are registered in `main.js`'s `UNITS`** as of Phase 97. Two conventions differ between them and are deliberate: Units 1–3's field records are real named documents, while **Units 4 through 8's are composite documents reconstructed from a documentary form** — a canal toll receipt, a Confederate impressment requisition, a land-office receiver's receipt, a ship's manifest page, a recorded deed with a restrictive covenant — because nobody standing on a towpath is carrying the Bank Veto. Each such citation says so in its own first sentence; the canonical documents live in `content/primary-source-library/` and feed the missions and Archive Challenges instead. See `docs/decision-log/0049-units-four-and-five.md`. **Unit 7 was registered for validation and deliberately absent from `main.js`'s `UNITS` between Phase 89 and Phase 89C** — because `activeFieldMap()` silently falls back to Unit 1's Caribbean for a unit it has no map for, so registering a field case early does not error, it lands the player on the wrong continent. That is the state Units 3–5 sat in until Phase 81F, Unit 6 until Phase 87, and Unit 8 from Phase 95 to 97; see `docs/decision-log/0075-unit-seven-exists-on-paper.md` and `0094`. **Don't register a future unit's field case before its map exists.** The serverless AI-grading backend at `api/evaluate.js` + `api/_lib/rubrics.js` (a real Claude-Haiku HIPP/SAQ/LEQ/DBQ evaluator — it's why `@anthropic-ai/sdk` is a production dependency) was unwired from the frontend for a long time; **as of Phase 22 it is wired up**, called from `sourceReader()`'s "initial reading" and the Archive Review SAQ block via `apps/web/src/engine/evaluator-requests.js`/`evaluator-client.js`. It still only ever returns formative feedback, never a grade — a teacher enters the real grade via the new `gradingScreen()`.

### State and persistence

`apps/web/src/engine/chronicle-progress-store.js` defines `DEFAULT_PROGRESS` (current screen, unlocked/completed cases, per-case evidence, dialogue responses, exchange ledger, empire connections, review answers, quest responses, `submissions`, `lastSavedAt`, etc.) and reads/writes it to `localStorage` under the key `republic-builder.chronicle.unit-01.v2`. **As of Phase 22, `localStorage` is no longer the only copy of a signed-in student's save** — `apps/web/src/repositories/progress-repository.js` sits in front of the untouched `local-progress-repository.js`/`chronicle-progress-store.js` and debounce-pushes to a real Supabase backend in the background (no-op if signed out), with a `hydrateRemoteProgress()` step merging a remote copy in on sign-in (last-write-wins by `lastSavedAt`). `localStorage` remains the fast/offline-first path and the only copy for anyone not signed into a classroom. `main.js` accesses this via `loadProgress` / `saveProgress` / `resetProgress` / `hasSavedProgress` / `hydrateRemoteProgress` exported from `progress-repository.js`. `progress.currentScreen` plus `VALID_SCREENS` in `main.js` drive the screen-routing state machine — examples include `institute`, `field`, `archive`, `practice-check`, `join`, `login`, `teacher-dashboard`, `grading`, and many others (30+ screens total).

Real accounts/classrooms/submissions/grades now also live in Supabase (schema + RLS in `supabase/migrations/0001_init.sql`) — `profiles`, `classrooms`, `roster_slots`, `student_world_profiles` (the remote mirror of the `progress` blob above), `submissions`, `evaluations`, `manual_grades`, `content_overrides`. See `docs/architecture/session-reports/2026-07-18-real-teacher-mode-accounts-classrooms-grading.md` for the full design and what's still unverified against a real project.

### Engine vs. content boundary

The repo's stated architecture rule (from the decision log): **engine code never contains APUSH-specific facts.** In practice today this is still violated — case-ID literals (`"case-001"`) are hard-coded directly into movement/interaction-gating code in `main.js`, not merely into content files. Treat the clean separation described in older docs as aspirational for the current vertical slice, not yet fully realized. One of these was removed in Phase 70 and the way it went is the pattern to follow: `sourceAvailability()`'s `caseId === "case-001"` branch became a `requiresSourceId` field the record itself carries, paid for by the second consumer that needed the same gate rather than by a speculative cleanup. **When a second case needs behaviour a literal currently hard-codes for the first, make it data instead of adding a second literal.** Canonical folder intent (corrected against the actual repository — the old table here previously had the asset-tree row backwards):

| Thing                                          | Home                                                                                                      |
| ---------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Reusable engine systems                        | `apps/web/src/engine/`                                                                                    |
| Campaign/unit content actually used at runtime | `apps/web/src/content/` — `unit-01-` through `unit-05-campaign.js`, all real                              |
| Images, maps, audio, icons                     | `apps/web/src/assets/` — referenced via `new URL(..., import.meta.url)`; `npm run docs:stats` counts them |
| JSON schemas                                   | `data/schemas/` — one example instance, no reader (see below)                                             |
| Docs                                           | `docs/`                                                                                                   |
| Build/import/validation scripts                | `scripts/`                                                                                                |

The placeholder-scaffold root `assets/` tree and the orphaned `apps/web/src/features/` island named in older revisions of this table were deleted in a dead-code-removal pass — see `docs/migrations/DEAD-CODE-REMOVAL.md`.

`data/schemas/` is **not a real JSON Schema and has no reader** — 11 lines, and nothing in `apps/web/src`, `scripts/` or `tests/` loads it, nor does `data/sample-saves/`. The live schemas are the Zod ones in `apps/web/src/content/schemas/`, which `npm run validate:content` actually runs. `THIRD-PARTY-TOOLING-AUDIT.md` justifies keeping `data/` as "a portable contract for the dormant `content/campaigns`/`content/library` JSON tree" — that tree is gone (see the paragraph below), so the stated reason no longer holds and the directory is a candidate for deletion rather than a documented intent.

There **was** a dormant, unread JSON content pipeline under `content/campaigns/chronicle/units/unit-01/` plus record templates in `content/library/`, and revisions of this file described it as a _fourth_ incompatible schema for the same Case 1.01 content. **It is gone.** The whole repo-root `content/` tree was deleted in `e715156` — `ls content` finds no such directory and `git ls-files | grep ^content/` returns nothing. This line is kept rather than dropped because the paragraph it replaces survived that deletion and sent readers looking for ten files that are not there. Schema reconciliation is still not a current task; it is tracked as future `ContentRegistry`/Zod work in `docs/architecture/PLATFORM-ARCHITECTURE-PROPOSAL.md`.

### Author Mode and real Teacher Mode (Phase 22 — no longer a stub)

A development-only in-app panel (toggled via the chrome button, rendered by `authorPanel()` in `main.js`) lets a teacher edit two front-facing copy fields (unit title, central question) without touching layout, scoring, or progression logic — see `docs/decision-log/0003-author-mode-and-content-overrides.md` for the original intent. **This is real and working**, not a stub: edits persist via `apps/web/src/repositories/teacher-override-repository.js`, which falls back to a per-browser `local-teacher-override-store.js` for signed-out/local dev, or writes to a classroom-scoped `content_overrides` Supabase table once a real teacher/classroom is active (Phase 22). (An earlier revision of this file claimed the two content-edit inputs had no working event listener — that was fixed in an earlier phase and this file simply hadn't been updated; don't trust old staleness claims about this panel without checking `main.js` directly.)

Beyond Author Mode's content overrides, Chronicle now has real teacher-facing tooling: a teacher creates a classroom, provisions a student roster, reviews AI-evaluated submissions, and enters manual grades via the `teacher-dashboard`/`grading` screens (`main.js`) backed by a real, live Supabase project (accounts, classrooms, submissions, evaluations, manual grades — schema in `supabase/migrations/0001_init.sql`). The **Manage Content** editor (`main.js`, `manageContentCaseScreen()`, reached from the Teacher Dashboard's Units tab — `manageContentScreen()` was a separate standalone screen but was folded into that tab before Phase 28 and no longer exists as of Phase 37) lets a teacher browse every mission grouped by unit, see what kind of mission it is (walkable map vs. activity vs. Archive-Challenge-only), and swap a curated alternate for any of a mission's editable slots (source, any of the 4 quest types, or a Case 1.02 Exchange Ledger record) with a draft/preview/publish step, backed by `classroom_content_selections` (`supabase/migrations/0006_teacher_mode.sql`, generalized to all quest types by `0007_generalize_content_slots.sql` and to ledger records by `0010_ledger_record_slots.sql`). A separate Teacher Dashboard Sources tab lets a teacher curate a classroom's pool of candidate sources per unit, backed by `classroom_unit_source_pool` (`0009_classroom_unit_source_pool.sql`). This is a real, bounded content-selection pipeline (pick from a small pre-authored pool per slot), **not** a from-scratch content-authoring tool — a teacher cannot write new question text/choices/answers through this screen; that remains a deferred future system. See `docs/architecture/session-reports/2026-07-18-real-teacher-mode-accounts-classrooms-grading.md` for the original design and `ARCHITECTURE-QUICKREF.md`'s Phase 22–24 and Phase 38 entries for current status — all migrations through `0010` are applied and live-verified as of Phase 38. This does **not** include an `Assignment`/due-date model, classroom analytics, or a general content draft/publish/versioning pipeline — those remain deferred per `PLATFORM-ARCHITECTURE-PROPOSAL.md`.

### Story canon (Phase 78, decision log `0061`)

The frame story lives in focused documents, not here. Read them before writing narrative, a cutscene, or Meridian art — and note that **ordinary historical uncertainty is never temporal drift**, which is the line all four exist to hold:

- `docs/design/CHRONICLE-CANON.md` — the five travel rules, the Original Drift, Chronicle vs. the Meridian Institute, banned phrasing, and which ideas are canonical / provisional / deferred.
- `docs/design/MAP-NARRATIVE-STRUCTURE.md` — Missions (locked map story) vs. **Field Assignments** (the six teacher-editable slots), and the Era Record as a derived view.
- `docs/design/THE-FIELD-LIAISON.md` and `docs/design/CUTSCENE-AND-DIALOGUE-CONVENTIONS.md` — the recurring helper, and what a scripted scene must tear down.
- `docs/art/MERIDIAN-VISUAL-IDENTITY.md` — Meridian's palette and the Liaison's PixelLab spec.

**Near-term architecture is deliberately minimal**, per the review: keep working code where it already lives (`main.js`, `content/*.js`) and add thin wrappers/tests/schemas around it rather than moving it. Concretely:

- **Vitest and Zod are the only approved immediate major dependencies.** Both are adopt-now, zero-POC-required.
- **Playwright is no longer deferred** — Phase 29 committed a real, rerunnable `npm run test:e2e` suite (`playwright.config.js`, `tests/e2e/*.spec.js`), now the largest suite in the repo, visual-regression baselines included (`npm run docs:stats` for current counts). Use it as the default verification path — see "Verification ladder" below. **Tiled is no longer fully deferred either** — `.tmj` rendering via `apps/web/src/engine/tiled-map-loader.js` is an established pattern for **twenty-one walkable surfaces** — eight outdoor field maps, ten field interiors and three Institute hub rooms, out of twenty-two committed `.tmj` files, the spare being Unit 9's ground with nobody on it. Run `npm run docs:stats` rather than quoting that number back: it is a count a new unit changes, and every figure in this sentence was wrong before Phase 118 checked it. This is still not license for a bigger Tiled-authoring pipeline. **Phaser and inkjs remain deferred** — real candidates for later, no concrete forcing function yet, no POC scheduled unless a task explicitly says otherwise.
- **`PlatformCore`, `WorldComposition`, runtime registries, activity-renderer registries, full subject-pack extraction (`packs/<subject>/`), accounts, classrooms, publishing, and any database** are documented future directions in the proposal above — not current implementation tasks. Don't scaffold them because the proposal describes them.
- **Do not create empty future-architecture folders** (`platform-core/`, `world-composition/`, `quest-engine/`, `runtime/`, `packs/`, etc.) "for structure." The repo already has a cautionary example of this exact mistake: `apps/web/src/features/{assessment,codex,character-creation}/` are empty `.gitkeep` folders from an earlier modularization attempt that a future reader has to investigate and discover are nothing. Don't add more.
- **Do not physically extract working movement, collision, camera, or NPC logic out of `main.js` merely for architectural neatness.** It works, it has no test coverage either way, and moving it is pure code-motion risk with no near-term payoff — per the review, add `export` to specific functions worth unit-testing and test them in place instead. Only physically extract this code if/when a proven Phaser adapter is actually replacing it, which is not scheduled.
- **Preserve the working game.** Every change should leave `npm run dev` producing identical player-visible behavior unless the task's explicit goal is new behavior — verify in the browser, not just via lint/build.

### Documentation conventions

## Terminology

Fixed internal vocabulary — use these exact terms in code, copy, and UI strings. **Each entry below is the rule; `docs/reference/INVARIANTS.md` holds the full text, including the incident history that argues it. Read that entry before changing code in its area.**

- **Chronicle Institute** — the organization. **Institute Archive** — the visible hub room/screen name.
- **Entrance Hall** — the room the player arrives in. One of **three** hub rooms, all on the `institute` screen, told apart by `progress.currentHubRoom` (`"main"` | `"archive"` | `"hallway"`). There is no `intro-hallway` screen; **don't reintroduce a screen for a room.**
- **Chronotravel** — traveling to a historical setting (the `travel` screen). **Only a case with a map travels.** The other two cases in every unit open directly on their own screen, because a plate belongs to a unit through its map and painting one place under the name of another is what happened for sixteen of twenty-four cases until Phase 115. Canon rule 3 is the licence: the Navigation Table opens a passage through a _strong_ imprint. See `docs/decision-log/0114-the-painting-was-of-somewhere-else.md`.
- **Chronotravel plate** — the painted establishing shot a warp screen loads on. **Keyed by unit, and exactly one case per unit opens on it** — the one that walks that unit's map. A plate's `note` is flavour and **must stay flavour** — never teach through it. A new unit costs one table line in `content/chronotravel-plates.js`.
- **Warp screen** — `travel` out and `return-warp` back, both built by `warpScreen()`. Full-bleed and chrome-less; three phases on one render, flipped via `data-warp-phase`. **A phase flip must never rebuild markup.** Second-beat timing is a CSS delay, not a class swap. The ring shows the dwell, not the bytes, and its `@property --warp-pct` **must declare `inherits: true`**. No tunnel canvas is emitted under `prefers-reduced-motion`. The screen never leaves on its own. Both recall paths play it.
- **Preservation Case** — the badge-case UI on the Archive trophy shelf. Styled like a Pokémon badge case, not a debug panel.
- **Navigation Table** — the Archive object the player walks to to pick a case/route. **Its period strip has four states and draws the one it is in** — selected, open, archived (`✓`, off `progress.completedUnits`) and teacher-locked. Mark a state inside the pill, never by widening the strip or by colour alone. **And navigation goes above the prose that varies**: the strip and its legend above the guiding question, the Chronotravel button above the case summary, and `.archive-copy` pinned to the top of its grid row rather than centred against the panel beside it. A control's position must not depend on how long an author wrote — that had put six period tabs and the button that starts the mission below the fold on Unit 7 at 1280×720, and it was measured clear twice on the one state that cannot show it.
- **Recall to Archive** — the field control returning the player to the Institute, landing beside the Navigation Table. **Two buttons, one action** (`field-recall`), but **only the beacon has a position** and is proximity-gated; the back link must never be gated. **That position is data and nothing in the stylesheet may set it** — `recallBeacon()` writes each map’s `FIELD_MAPS[unit].recall` as an inline style, and a `.recall-beacon { left/top }` rule with `!important` beats it: one stood in `global.css` from before the beacon was data-driven, so it was _drawn_ at a single fixed point on all eight maps while `isNearRecallBeacon()` kept measuring to the authored tile **16 to 23.5 tiles away**, against a reach of 1.55. The visible beacon refused every click and the place that worked had no marker. Guarded by `tests/e2e/recall-beacon.spec.js`, which walks to the beacon **as drawn** and lets the game’s own `is-near` say whether the walk arrived. See `docs/decision-log/0139-the-beacon-was-not-where-it-looked.md`. Anything repointing this must carry `exitPreviewIfActive()`. The beacon stays out of `nearestFieldInteraction()` deliberately.
- **Mission Tracker** — the field's objective panel. **One block**: a record row for the open mission takes that mission's name; an unopened record names the person carrying it. Don't reintroduce a name heading under the list.
- **Activity engine** — a playable interaction on the `activity` screen (`engine/activities/`). Four exist: `interview`, `assembly`, `discrepancy`, `trace`. **The engine key is registry key, screen id and `activityRoute` at once** — renaming one is a save-compatibility change. An engine holds **no APUSH facts**. Every authored field map has three missions; **read the slate off `THE-MAP-PROGRAM.md` §2's own table, never a prose summary**. **A unit must not repeat its neighbour's three engines**, and a repeated engine must be pointed at a different question. **An ASSEMBLY slot takes exactly one fragment** and every slot must be claimed. **Only a field case can host an activity.** **An activity states its own rules** — treat `howItWorks`/`terms`/`briefing` as defaults, not extras. **A record that is filed is filed**: every reducer refuses every verb that could un-complete it, and every renderer disables its own controls; INTERVIEW exempts `ask` and `log` only. `howItWorks` is a screen (Mission Instructions), not a panel — three steps, capped at four — and **the board's reference copy of it is collapsed**, with the glossary and the briefing, into three `<details>`; the `<summary>` keeps the screen's own heading, the open flag must live in `progress.settings` because `render()` rebuilds `#app` wholesale, and what stays open in their place is the question and **one objective line**. **All four engines declare `summary()`** as of Phase 109 — INTERVIEW alone did until then, so the Mission Tracker and that line were blank on seventeen of twenty-four missions — and **the activity screen states progress in exactly one place**, that line. An INTERVIEW's answer grid is **deliberately sparse** — two thirds of the game's 156 authored answers are flat on purpose; `asked` and `logged` are different lists; **`log` has three outcomes and `interviewLogReceipt()` is the one place they are told apart** — it owns each one's **mark** as well as its words, and the three are the game's own `✓` secured / `✦` available / `·` nothing here; **there is no `✗` in this game and a deflection is not a wrong answer** — while the offer above it stays one string under every authored answer; **a wrong choice says something on every engine** (one placeless line, engine-side, not content-overridable yet) and **right and wrong sound different** — `secure` when a press lands, `flat` when it does not or is refused, where landed means the engine's own ratio went up, an entry was kept, or the record just closed; `interviewTokens()` ids are **not unit-scoped**, so check new speaker ids against shipped ones. **Scope any new `.activity-shell`/`.activity-board` rule with `[data-activity-source]`.**
- **A verdict a teacher reads is not the string the evaluator returns.** The Archive Evaluator's `readiness` is an enum declared in `api/_lib/rubrics.js`'s JSON schema — written for a model, not a reader — and **`readinessLabel()` is the one place it becomes words.** Both sites call it: the Teacher Dashboard's Readiness column and `archiveFeedbackMarkup()`, itself shared by `sourceReader()`, `reviewScreen()` and `gradingScreen()`. The column rendered `sub.readiness` raw until Phase 145, so a teacher scanning a class read `needs_fresh_attempt` while the grading screen one click away read "Try a fresh attempt" — **the roster's STATUS column beside it had exactly this defect and was fixed, and this was its untouched twin.** A new verdict added to the schema is caught by `tests/unit/readiness-label.test.js`, which reads the enum back out of the `RUBRICS` export and asserts **a person's words have no underscores in them**. See `docs/decision-log/0144-the-list-spoke-in-database.md`. **And the same payload carries two more enums, which took until Phase 150** (`0149`): the evaluator answers in two shapes, and `archiveFeedbackMarkup()` de-underscored a HIPP reading's `elements` while printing a rubric row's `${row.met}` raw — so a student read **`PART C — NOT_YET`** about their own writing, uppercased by the stylesheet, and the `rows` shape is **everything except the source reader** — every unit's Archive Review SAQ and every SAQ and DBQ Archive Challenge, which is all the extended writing in the game. `RUBRIC_ROW_LABELS`/`rubricRowLabel()` and `RUBRIC_MET_LABELS`/`rubricMetLabel()` sit beside `READINESS_LABELS` for the same reason and fall through the same way, and `tests/unit/rubric-row-labels.test.js` reads both enums back out of `RUBRICS` — aimed at **a new DBQ row**, since that rubric is the one that grows. It survived two passes at its own twin because **no fixture in the repository had ever held a rubric row**: the suite's one SAQ feedback blob was written in the _HIPP_ shape, with `element` values in neither schema's enum, under a comment claiming it was the SAQ schema's. A fixture that states a shape is not evidence of it.
- **An assignment names a task, and a task id is not a quest id.** An assignment is `(task_type, task_id)` and `computeAssignmentReport()` matches a submission on both — and until Phase 151 (`0150`) `task_id` was a **free-text box** whose placeholder was an Archive Challenge's **quest id**, a string nothing in the game records. The four sites that record one all agree with each other: a source reading is `source.id`, a unit's Archive Review is `saq-${unit.id}`, and an Archive Challenge is `${questType}-quest-${questId}`. A teacher who followed the example got a confident `0/N submitted` forever, with no error, on the screen they use to decide who is behind — **the hazard being already written down three lines above about the field beside it**, where `ASSIGNABLE_TASK_TYPES` excludes `leq` for precisely it. Every legal id is derivable, so `assignableTasks()` derives them (59 today) and **the type comes with the task** rather than being a second control that can disagree. A record with a `readerQuestType` is excluded because it never reaches the evaluator. **A Teacher Mode swap does not affect a task id** — `resolveSourceSlot()`/`resolveQuestSlot()` return `{ ...alt, id: officialSource.id }`, so a slot keeps its id whatever a classroom published. Rows created before the picker still exist and some name nothing, so such a row carries a **Matches no assessment** chip rather than being silently rewritten. Guarded by `tests/unit/assignable-tasks.test.js`, which holds the picker against the **request builders in `evaluator-requests.js`** — where a `taskId` is actually decided — rather than against a copy of the rule.
- **A slot has an id, and its label is not it.** Evidence-organizing is the only quest type whose content cross-references itself — `source.correctSlotId` names the slot a record belongs in, and **that string is the graded answer**. It did not survive Manage Content's editor: `evidenceOrganizingToFields()` kept only the label and `buildEvidenceOrganizingContent()` re-derived the id as `slugify(label)`, while every authored quest writes a short id beside a long label (`agriculture-diet` for "Transformed Agriculture and Diet"). In the browser that is worse than a dangling reference: each source row's Correct slot is a `<select>` whose options are those same slugs, so it **cannot display a value it has no option for and falls back to its first**, `syncAuthoringFieldsFromDom()` reads that back as the teacher's own choice, and the builder's own fallback coerces anything unmatched to slot 0 — so nothing objects. **Opening Case 1.02's editor and pressing Publish without touching anything filed three of its four records under the wrong slot**, for a class. Fixed in Phase 149 (`0148`) by carrying the id: `slotIdOf(row)` is `row.id || slugify(row.label)`, the slot row carries `data-slot-id` and the sync **reads it back rather than re-deriving it**, and `addEvidenceSlot()` mints one at birth — without that a new slot's identity is `slugify("")`, which every other unnamed slot also has and which moves the moment a name is typed. **`slugify()`-as-id had exactly one load-bearing use and this was it**: `shortId()` mints a fresh unique id for sequencing items, MCQ choices, HIPP prompts and evidence _sources_, and none of those is pointed at by anything. Guarded by `tests/unit/custom-content-authoring.test.js` and `tests/e2e/teacher-content-reaches-students.spec.js`, which also holds Teacher Mode's two end-to-end promises — the unit gate opening a period for the class, and a published wording reaching the student's board.
- **Mission** vs. **Archive Challenge** — load-bearing distinction. A mission is one case reached by Chronotravel, using the four swappable quest types. An Archive Challenge is a unit's extended written work (`saq`, `dbq`) from the Archive Terminal. Don't put a swappable type in `archiveChallenges[]` or an SAQ/DBQ in a case slot.
- **A finished quest renders as a record, and the record is sealed at both ends.** `renderQuest(type, quest, state, { readOnly: true })` — every renderer disables its own controls **and** `isSealedQuestTarget()` refuses in the four handlers, off `data-quest-readonly` on the quest root. Textareas go `readonly`, not `disabled`. There is no submit step in this codebase, so an editable finished quest re-grades itself. Applied to the completed mission only; the Archive Challenge and the Practice Check stay editable on purpose.
- **A mission has exactly one name** — the name half of `case.title` via `resolvedCaseName()`, and **every heading that names a mission calls that and not `resolvedCaseTitle()`**, which is the number joined to the name. The case number is an eyebrow, never inside a heading. The two functions differ **only on Unit 1**, so the wrong one looks right on twenty-four cases out of twenty-seven. `case.mechanic` is teacher-side help text. `case.shortTitle` means the _place_ and is not rename-aware.
- **A case says where and when it is set, once.** `caseWhereAndWhen()` joins `location` and `date`, and `location` is authored either as a bare place or as a place that already carries its own date. **Append only when the location names no year** — asking whether it contains _this_ date is a proxy that holds until a case's location carries the day a document was delivered and its `date` carries the span, which is how Unit 8 shipped "The United States Senate · 1 June 1950 · 1947–1954". **A guard that shares its subject's assumption is not a second opinion**: the e2e check written for the same defect asked whether a year _repeated_ and passed on that string every run. Its unit guard reads all nine registered units, not the eight in `UNITS`. See `docs/decision-log/0107-the-duplication-was-not-a-repeat.md`.
- **Field interior** — a room a field map opens into, declared on `FIELD_MAPS[unitId].interiors`, told apart by `progress.currentFieldRoom`. **An interior declares exactly the fields an outdoor map declares** — `id`, `grid`, `isLand`, `blocks`, `roads`, `npcs`, `behaviours`, `sourcePoints`, `worldMarkup` and, since Phase 137, `renderMap`; that shape-equality is the design. **A surface paints itself**: `render()` calls `activeFieldMap().renderMap?.()` and nothing else, where it used to carry nineteen hand-written `if`s. A surface missing `renderMap` does not error — `activeFieldOutdoorMap()` falls back to Unit 1, so `?.()` is a no-op and the surface paints an **empty frame** with the cast and the records drawn over it; guarded per surface, interiors included, in `field-map-coordinates.test.js`. Not a screen, not a portal graph, never nested. **A doorway is a column, not a gap** — a hole in an interior partition is only a door if the whole column above it is clear, and a room with interior walls is flood-filled after every furniture move.
- **A prop's size is the pack's opinion, not the world's.** Small props are drawn to fill their 48px tile; don't measure a new one against its neighbours. **Adding a sheet to a palette means adding its `import.meta.glob` in `main.js` too** — a missing one renders the whole map as an empty frame.
- **Hub object markers** are one class, `.hub-marker`, sized from each target's `marker` rect. Move an object in a generator, move its `marker` too. **Restyle `.hub-marker` in place; appending an override layer is how three competing styles came to exist.**
- Unit 1 badge areas: **Caribbean**, **Atlantic**, **Hispaniola** — all real, all playable in sequence.
- Institute NPCs: Director Rowan Hale, Dr. Amani Soto, Professor Julian Park — `director`, `amani`, `julian` in code.
- **Field Liaison** — Emery Voss, she/her. **The registry key is `liaison`, never the name.** Player-facing she is "Emery Voss" and **never her job title**. `progress.story.liaisonTrust` selects her line. She names Meridian in exactly one place, the `meridian-reveal` scene; Units 1–5 keep their field lines exactly as authored and only Unit 6 carries a `revealedText`. **Two costumes, one id** — `sheetFor()` is the single swap point. Nothing about the reveal is curricular.
- **A person the open mission still wants something from wears a mark, and there is one set of marks.** Record carriers and an open interview's cast share `.npc-source-badge` and the same `✦` available / `✓` secured — secured means a _useful answer logged_, a carried record wins, a locked one falls through, and the badge is `aria-hidden` so the state goes in the button's name. Don't pin a global badge count in a spec seeding a live interview. **The Mission Tracker's bar is one pip per thing wanted**, not a proportion. Neither is covered by any visual baseline.
- **A stationed body is furniture to the NPCs and a wall to the player.** Post new stations off the spawn's cardinals and out of corridors, then run `field-map-coordinates` and the field e2e specs.

## Gameplay invariants (regression-prone areas)

These recurred as bugs across many hotfix milestones. Be deliberate here and **re-test visually rather than trusting a syntax check.** As above, each line is the rule and `docs/reference/INVARIANTS.md` carries the case history — which is usually a record of the same bug shipping twice, and is the part that tells you which fix is the wrong one.

- **Camera must stay a pure function of player position.** No `scrollIntoView()`, no `.focus()`-triggered scrolling, no click-handler that moves the camera toward a clicked element.
- **Proximity-gated interaction.** Both `E` and click require the player already be in range — never teleport-and-interact.
- **One interaction prompt at a time**, cleared on close, on moving out of range, on screen change, and on reset. The field's `#fieldNotice` is a **status line, not a prompt**, and **an attempt that lands must clear the line left by one that missed** — and **it must be on screen**, which means above the guiding question rather than under the per-unit intro that had put it 445–648px below the fold on five of the eight maps.
- **Prose scrolls; the map, the status line and the button that starts a mission do not.** Phase 117's rule — _where a control sits does not depend on how long an author wrote_ — is not only the Navigation Table's. `.case-field--living` was `align-items: center`, so the playable map was centred against a column ending in `copy.intro`: Unit 1 opened with all 517px of the frame on screen and **Unit 7 with 283**, a 306px slide. It is `start` now and the frame's top edge takes one value on every unit. Mission Instructions had the same defect from the other side — `0054` put "Begin the mission" in the giver's column to keep it above the fold and the giver's own line then grew from 0 to 683 characters underneath it, so the button went **195px under** on Units 6 and 7 — and the button is placed against the portrait and caption now, with the line following it. Guarded at **both** 1280×720 and 1366×768 by `tests/e2e/fold-and-controls.spec.js`, which chooses its worst mission by measuring the content rather than by naming one. **A long board, a long quiz and a long record still scroll on purpose** — this is about controls and status, not about page height. See `docs/decision-log/0120-prose-scrolls-controls-do-not.md`.
- **And nothing is cut off the side of the screen, which is a different question from the fold.** A page that is too tall gets a scrollbar and says so; **a box that is too wide inside an `overflow: hidden` ancestor says nothing at all** — it is cut cleanly and looks designed that way. `.case-field--living` is a three-column grid whose own floors add up to 1268px (240 + 760 + 220 and two 24px gaps) against the 1209px `main.case-field` has at 1280 wide, so the Evidence Channel — the panel holding **Open Codex** and **Practice Check** — hung **59px off the screen on every one of the eight maps**, losing the last characters of every line and 35px off the right of both buttons. The threshold is **1339px of viewport**, which takes in 1280×720 and 1280×800 and misses 1366×768, where every visual baseline is taken. Fixed by one `@media (max-width: 1338px)` block lowering the two floors that do not fit — **scoped under the measured threshold on purpose**, because those floors are binding at 1366 and lowering them there would redistribute the grid and move eight baselines for a defect that is not present. Guarded at both sizes across nine student screens by `tests/e2e/horizontal-clipping.spec.js`, which measures each box against its nearest clipping ancestor and excludes only the camera windows (`.field-viewport`, `.institute-map`, the Archive Room's map — a world bigger than its frame is the point) and the inside of an `<svg>` (a `<path>`'s client rect is geometry in a viewBox, not layout). See `docs/decision-log/0123-cut-off-the-side-of-the-screen.md`. **And the same cut one level in: a box does not cut off its own contents either** (Phase 131, decision log `0130`) — an element that clips its own overflow and whose `scrollHeight`/`scrollWidth` exceeds what it shows, which is the ordinary fate of a fixed-height box whose text grew. Asked on the same page visits by the same spec, so it costs nothing extra, and **watched fail** at `quest-prompt cuts its own text by 71px tall`. Two exclusions: `text-overflow: ellipsis` is a truncation that **announces itself**, and a box too small to show a glyph at all is the screen-reader pattern (`.visually-hidden` is 1×1 and overflows itself by definition) — excluded by size rather than by class name. A sweep of twenty-six student screens at both sizes found **zero** of these, and zero truncated with a visible ellipsis, which is the null result Phase 129 needed: moving every text metric in the game at once truncated nothing. Note that `intro-sequence.spec.js` guards the only other fixed-height text box in the game, the Director's. See `docs/decision-log/0130-a-box-does-not-cut-its-own-text.md`.
- **And a new screen opens at its own top, while the screen you are on does not move under you.** `render()` replaces `#app` wholesale and the browser carries the document's scroll offset across that, clamped to what the new page can hold — so the closing screen of every mission in the game opened at its bottom. A player works down a **3,320px** interview board to the closer at its foot, standing at **2,600**; the Mission Debrief that opens on their click is **1,462px**, so they arrive at **742, its exact bottom**, with the mission's name, the record they just filed and the paragraph saying why it holds all above the window — and **nothing on the screen to press**, both controls being above it too (the onward button at −291, the back link at −606). The closer is below the fold on all four engines and every one of the twenty-four missions. **Both halves of the rule are load-bearing and they pull against each other**: every press on a board rebuilds `#app`, so a blanket `scrollTo(0, 0)` in `render()` throws a student ordering a sequencing row four screens down back to the heading. The reset is therefore keyed to **what is on screen**, and the screen id is not that key — Mission Instructions, the board and the debrief are three views behind one id, so `activityScreen()` writes `activityView` and the key is read after the switch. Guarded by `tests/e2e/arrival-scroll.spec.js`, watched fail at **742/121** with the reset disabled and at **0 where it wanted 1,234** with the reset made unconditional. Note that `source-reader-questions`, a committed visual baseline, **was a photograph of this defect** and had defended it since 2026-08-02. See `docs/decision-log/0124-a-new-screen-opens-at-its-top.md`.
- **And nothing is drawn as a box with nothing in it.** `.feedback` paints a full-width bar unconditionally, in `rgba(91, 150, 101, 0.2)` — **the same green `.feedback.success` declares** — and three of them ship empty in the markup because `showFeedback()` finds its target by id and the element must exist before it has anything to say. So Record Reconstruction, the Archive Review and the screen where a new player names their Chronicler each opened with a filled bar under their main button, in the colour this interface uses to say a thing went right, reading as a disabled button or an input that failed to load. Fixed by `.feedback:empty { display: none }` — **the same idiom and argument as `.director-reveal-rail:empty`**, which the stylesheet already uses, and preferred over moving the green onto `.success` because the join and login screens render `class="feedback"` with content and no type class and would have lost their panel. Removing the bar also lifted **Confirm identity →**, the button that starts the game, from 30px below the fold to fully on screen at 1366×768; at 1280×720 it is still 31px under, which is Phase 121's rule on a screen `fold-and-controls.spec.js` does not cover, and is recorded rather than chased. Guarded by `tests/e2e/empty-panels.spec.js` across twenty-six student screens, where **a panel is a filled box or a box with four edges and one edge is a line** — the Navigation Table's `.route-thread` is a 2px gold `border-top` and a connector is supposed to be empty — plus the counter-assertion that a line with something to say is still drawn, because hiding `.feedback` outright passes the whole sweep and silences every message in the game. See `docs/decision-log/0125-a-box-with-nothing-in-it.md`.
- **And a guard that seeds twice on one page is measuring the first seed twice.** `seedProgress()` installs an init script that writes only when the storage key is still empty — deliberate, because it is what lets `reloadIntoSave()` exist without clobbering the game's own writes — and **booting the game once saves**, so every later call on the same page declines to write and `loadSeededSave()` re-opens the first save. A spec looping over eight units on one page therefore boots case-001 eight times, and every assertion passes. All three of Phase 121's guards in `fold-and-controls.spec.js` sat like that from Phase 121 to Phase 127, including the one whose whole claim is that **the map frame's top edge takes the same value on all eight maps** — which one map satisfies trivially. Proved two-sided: with `align-items: center` put back on `.case-field--living`, the map-frame guard is **2 passed in 19.2s** seeding the old way and **2 failed** the new way (Unit 3 at 781 > 720, Unit 4 at 797 > 768) in 84s, the runtime itself being a tell. Use **`openSeededSave()`** in a loop — it writes the key directly on a page that has an origin, falls back to `seedProgress()` on one that does not, and then loads; `non-field-missions.spec.js` had been doing exactly that by hand since Phase 108 and nothing else could find it. All three Phase 121 guards **pass** once the seeding is real, so the fix they defend was always right; the defence was not. See `docs/decision-log/0126-the-loop-measured-one-unit-eight-times.md`.
- **And a control must be on the screen it belongs to, on every screen and not only on the field.** `fold-and-controls.spec.js`'s tables were the eight field cases and their missions, so the second screen of the game was uncovered — and it was wrong: the identity screen put **Confirm identity →**, the button that starts the game, **31px below the fold at 1280×720**, with `Return to protocol` beside it. It says one thing twice about one control — "Choose your appearance" above the two portraits and "Choose the field sprite that feels most like your Chronicler." below them — and the second sentence is exactly what does not fit. The two became one line above the control, **51 words on the screen where there were 55**, which is the owner's own rule that a clarity gain is paid for by removing text; the layout alternative was rejected because that padding belongs to `completion-shell` and moving it would move the four intro screens to fix one. Now 14px of clearance at 1280×720, 54 at 1366×768. The guard's third table is every screen whose controls must be reachable without scrolling, at both sizes, plus unit completion and the transmission across all eight units — **and the rule is still Phase 121's, prose scrolls and controls do not**: the Codex is 838px tall and passes because its one control is at the top, while the Practice Check, the Archive Review, every board, the debrief and the reader are excluded as things to read, and `travel`/`return-warp` are excluded because their controls are pinned to the bottom of the viewport and could not fail. **The Archive Rotation is excluded with its numbers written down** — "Next →" is 17px under on each MCQ, 115 on the sequencing item and 610 on the HIPP, and it moves further down when you answer — because a fix that clears three items and misses two is not the rule.
- **Dialogue renders anchored to the speaking NPC**, without resetting the world transform or scrolling the document. Content goes inside `.field-speech-bubble__scroll` or it will not scroll. **Check the cascade before the arithmetic here** — two rules in this area were dead for a full phase and were found by measuring in the browser, not by reading the file.
- **A per-frame path patches the DOM; it does not render — and it does not save.** The second half is Phase 143's and needs its own counter, because a save touches no DOM and the render observer cannot see one: with `save()` inserted into `runFieldMovementLoop()`, **both render cases pass and only the save case fails**. It matters because `saveProgress()` is a synchronous `localStorage.setItem` of the whole progress blob, so a save on the per-frame path stalls the main thread every frame and presents exactly like a busy machine. Currently **zero writes across six seconds of held keys** (control: one Recall press writes one 1,217-byte blob), watched failing at 22 and 24. **The audit's `saveProgress` debounce item is retired on that measurement** — the only `save()` reachable from the loop is in `closeFieldDialogueOnMove()`, which nulls `progress.activeFieldNpc` and so cannot fire on a second consecutive frame. See `docs/decision-log/0142-and-it-does-not-save.md`, which also records the loop's real per-second cost: **1,337 DOM queries walking, not the audit's ~2,600**, 85 forced layouts (the audit was right), and — the part it never reported — **518 DOM queries and 61 player style rewrites per second while standing perfectly still**, because `updateFieldNpcs()` ends by calling `updateFieldPlayer()` on an uncleared 33 ms interval. Trimming that idle half touches movement feel and is the owner's call, not a change to slip in beside a test. Making the movement loop `render()` repaints map canvases mid-walk and turns a performance regression into four red specs. That used to be caught only by walks parking short — a signal that fires just as readily for a busy laptop, which is why Phase 93 gave the claim its own test: `tests/e2e/frame-budget.spec.js` counts wholesale `#app` re-renders during a walk and **does not care how fast the machine is**. Several specs failing at once in an untouched area is still one cause rather than several flakes; check the frame-budget spec first, because it names the cause.
- **And where a path does render per frame, nothing inside it may use a CSS clock.** Storm Navigation is the one place in the game that rebuilds a container's whole `innerHTML` every animation frame — **121 rebuilds in 2 seconds, measured** — so every node in it is new about every 16ms. An `animation` restarts its timeline before it can advance and a `transition` has no previous value to ease from, and **both fail silently**: a frozen animation is indistinguishable from a decoration that was never meant to move. `.storm-clouds` carried `animation: stormCloudsDrift 14s linear infinite` from the day the mini-game shipped and **the sky never drifted once**, under a storm where the water, rain, lightning and ship all moved — because those were computed from `state.elapsedMs` and written inline, which is what `storm-navigation.js`'s own file header prescribes and what the clouds were never brought under. A value that must change over time is computed from `elapsedMs` and emitted inline. **Key this to Storm alone** — Cargo Sorting shares the loop and the container but redraws only when its displayed second changes, **1 rebuild in 2 seconds**, so its `transition` completes fine and a sweep over "mini-games" would answer its question with Storm's answer. Guarded statically by `tests/unit/storm-navigation-css-clock.test.js` and in the browser by `tests/e2e/storm-clouds.spec.js`. **And you cannot hold a handle to anything in that stage**: `locator.evaluate()` reads a detached node often enough to report a moving sky as a still one (measured: `transform: ""`, client width `0`), so sample from inside one `page.evaluate` and treat a missing transform as a failure, never as a zero. See `docs/decision-log/0140-the-sky-never-moved.md`.

A record's availability is `sourceAvailability()`'s answer; **do not add a case-id literal beside it.** The dual is equally load-bearing: **when one variable answers two questions, the second question gets the first one's answer** — hence `codexOrigin` beside `sourceOrigin`.

The walk assertion could **not** be broken by moving a door into the middle of its own 2×2 footprint or by stationing a body on the door cell, because the reach is 1.45 tiles and these doors sit on small buildings on open streets; it is kept for a doorstep genuinely walled off and is not sensitive. **And a stationed NPC's position comes from the unit's `*_NPC_BEHAVIOURS` table, not the `x`/`y` on its `*_FIELD_NPCS` entry** — editing the latter moves nobody while `__chronicleCast()` reports the behaviour's coordinate back at you, which reads exactly like a stale dev server and is not one. See `docs/decision-log/0141-every-room-can-be-walked-into.md`.

  - **A door's row comes off the generated `*_DOORS` export, never off the generator's constants by hand.**

  - **A room smaller than its frame centres; it does not clamp.**

Guarded by `tests/e2e/cast-nameplates.spec.js`, which measures the layer **in pixels** — a z-index read back would restate the rule, and `elementFromPoint` answers about clicks. Guarded by `tests/e2e/fold-and-controls.spec.js`, which could not reach either screen before — `FIXED_SCREENS` seeds a `progress.currentScreen` and the landing is `showMainMenu` module state.

- **One input lock, `isHubInputLocked()`**, checked at the same three sites. Anything that locks movement must also suppress the interaction prompt. Keep the function even though it is one term.
- **A body you are already standing inside does not block you.** `isBlockedByBody()`'s `here` clause, which the hub has had since Phase 64 and the field took only in Phase 112 — without it an overlap has no exit, and `exitFieldInterior()` restores `fieldReturn` verbatim, so the game can put the player inside a stationed body unaided. The player's, the NPC's-own and the NPC-blocking foot boxes are **three different widths on purpose**; don't reconcile them to fix a snag.

## Visual design language

- Palette (see CSS custom properties in `apps/web/src/styles/global.css`): deep navy (`--navy`), gold (`--gold`, `--gold-soft`), warm parchment/ink (`--paper`, `--ink`), muted teal accents. Keep new UI within this blue/gold/bronze/parchment historical-adventure look, not a generic admin-panel style.
- **The web fonts are asked for in one request, so one malformed family loses every one of them** (Phase 129, decision log `0128`). `global.css`'s first line is a Google Fonts `@import` — **Cinzel**, **DM Sans**, **Spectral**, and since Phase 130 **EB Garamond**. It named two axes for DM Sans — `opsz,wght` — and then supplied one pair and three loose numbers, which the `css2` API answers **400 Bad Request**; the rule is that _every tuple after the `@` carries a value for every axis named before it_. Shipped in the second commit of the project and never once corrected, so for 312 commits the whole game drew in `serif`, `system-ui` and `sans-serif`. **It is silent three ways and every one of them matters here**: the 400 comes back as `text/html`, which Chromium refuses to hand a stylesheet request, so it surfaces as `net::ERR_BLOCKED_BY_ORB` and never as a status; a font that does not arrive is a fallback rather than an error, so nothing throws; and all sixty-one visual baselines were **recorded** in the fallback faces, so the suite defended the defect exactly as `0124` §7 records `source-reader-questions` doing. **`document.fonts.check()` will tell you the font is fine and it is lying** — it returns `true` for a family that does not exist. Measure a string in the family against the same string in its fallback, or read `document.fonts.size`. Fixing it moved every text metric in the game and **every geometry guard still passed**; the one screen that did not was the Navigation Table, whose left column now floors at 275px because at 230 the lead sentence took two lines and the period strip took a fourth row. See `docs/decision-log/0128-the-game-has-never-seen-its-own-fonts.md`.
- **A face has a role, and the one a question is read in is `--c-font-prompt`** (Phase 130, decision log `0129`). Four roles, and three of them are chrome: `--c-font-display` (Spectral, headings), `--c-font-label` (**Cinzel**, eyebrows/pills/buttons), `--c-font-ui` (DM Sans, body and controls), and `--c-font-prompt` (**EB Garamond**, the one element a student has to read closely). `.quest-prompt` had hard-coded `"Cinzel", serif` at 1.05rem, bypassing the tokens — and Cinzel is the **label** face: of its 48 sized uses, **44 are under 0.9rem**. So every AP stem in the game was set in small capitals by the face drawn for three-word buttons, on the element **all five quest renderers share** with the sealed archive-challenge record. It carries its own size token, `--c-text-prompt: 1.15rem`, because Garamond's x-height is small and a straight swap reads a size down — **measured, not guessed**: one stem sets 703px in Cinzel at 1.05rem, 666px in Garamond at the same size and 730px at 1.15. **Change the two tokens together.** And because the families share one request, **adding or editing a family means verifying the URL as it sits on disk returns `200 text/css` with every family in the response** — order does not matter (both orders measured 200), tuples do. See `docs/decision-log/0129-a-question-is-read-not-labelled.md`.
- A custom bronze/gold ornate arrow cursor is already implemented as an inline SVG data URI in `global.css` — reuse/extend it rather than reverting to the system cursor for in-game surfaces.
- Don't cluster interactive elements together while leaving large map areas empty — placement should be diegetic (e.g. cartographer table near the ship, canoe worker near shore) and UI chrome (status panels, prompts) should sit outside the playable floor rather than overlapping NPCs, furniture, or pathways.

## Persistence

- **A save belongs to a student, not to the machine, and ownership is asked before the timestamps** (Phase 148, `0147`). `progress` is one flat blob under one key, and until this phase nothing in it said whose it was — so `resolveProgressConflict()` did the only thing it could and compared `lastSavedAt` against the remote copy's `updated_at`. On a cart Chromebook that rotates between students every period, **the newest save is reliably the one the last student left**, so it won. Measured end to end through the join flow: the machine's save reached **two different students' cloud rows**, and a returning student signing in with their own correct password had **three completed cases replaced by their classmate's one** — in `student_world_profiles`, the copy that follows them to another device. `progress.ownerUserId` is stamped by `hydrateRemoteProgress()`, which main.js wires to `onAuthStateChange` as well as to boot, and a save owned by somebody else **is not a candidate however recent it is**: the remote copy is taken, or `null` — start clean — when this student has none. **An unowned save is still absorbed on purpose**; it is the only way progress made before signing in ever reaches an account. **And the second half is not optional**, because signing in is two independent async paths: the handler that sets the screen and calls `save()` beats the hydrate, so `saveProgress` captures the pre-sign-in blob and pushes it two seconds later to the row of whoever just signed in — **a save is only ever pushed to the row of the student it belongs to**, guarded at the push. With ownership alone, the returning student got his game back locally and his cloud row held an empty save. Note that the file already knew the risk and had guarded the wrong thing: `progress-repository.js`'s header says it exists because classrooms mean shared Chromebooks, and `remote-auth-repository.js` clears its profile cache on a different user signing in for exactly this reason — the profile was protected and the save was not. **Two things here are the owner's call and were left alone**: a student **cannot sign out** (`signOut()` has one caller in the app, `teacher-sign-out`, rendered only on the teacher dashboard) and nothing tells a student whose account they are in; and a teacher's save is unowned too, so a machine a teacher demonstrated on still hands its save on, which is the solo-absorb feature rather than a separate defect. Guarded by `tests/unit/progress-repository-conflict.test.js` and `tests/e2e/classroom-lifecycle.spec.js`.
- **And an uncontrolled form is emptied by the render that refuses it.** The join and teacher sign-in inputs are read from the DOM at submit time — `passwordFieldMarkup()`'s own comment says so, and skips `render()` on the show/hide toggle for that reason — but the `render()` that draws an error wiped them, so a student who mistyped a password had to re-enter the classroom code and student ID they had just been handed on a slip of paper. `authUiState.signInDraft` is the same answer the teacher **signup** wizard has always had in `signupDraft`; passwords are deliberately not kept, and the draft is cleared on success as well, because leaving one student's code in the form for the next person is the same mistake as leaving them their save.

`chronicle-progress-store.js` (key `republic-builder.chronicle.unit-01.v2`) is still the base layer: a single flat `localStorage` blob, and the only save state for anyone not signed into a real classroom. **As of Phase 22, this is no longer the only copy for a signed-in student**: a real Supabase backend now exists (accounts, classrooms, submissions, evaluations, manual grades, classroom-scoped content overrides — schema in `supabase/migrations/0001_init.sql`), and `apps/web/src/repositories/progress-repository.js` layers background sync on top of the untouched local store — exactly the "small service/adapter in front of a versioned save schema" this section used to describe as future design intent. When adding new persisted fields, still extend `DEFAULT_PROGRESS` and the merge logic in `readProgress()` — that pattern is unchanged, just now also mirrored to `student_world_profiles` in Supabase. See `docs/architecture/session-reports/2026-07-18-real-teacher-mode-accounts-classrooms-grading.md` for the full design and what's still unverified against a live project.

## Development workflow expectations

- Prefer small, focused changes over broad refactors; don't fix unrelated things in the same pass.
- Avoid modifying `vite.config.js` unless the task genuinely requires it.
- Compilation/syntax passing is not sufficient to call something fixed — this is a visual/interactive game. Verify using the ladder below before considering a change done. Don't commit until the requested behavior has actually been tested.
- **No `/verify` skill exists in this repo** — an earlier revision of this file referenced one; don't look for it or invent an ad hoc equivalent. The verification ladder below is the actual expectation.

### Verification ladder (cheapest first — escalate only as needed)

Run the cheapest check that actually exercises what you changed; stop once you've cleared the tier that matters for this change. Don't jump straight to a manual/MCP browser pass by default — it is the _most_ expensive tier, not the default one.

1. **Targeted `vitest`**, scoped to the changed file/pattern: `npx vitest run tests/unit/<file>.test.js`, not a bare `npm run test`.
2. **`npm run validate:content`** — only if content files changed (schemas, `unit-0N-campaign.js`, `content/quests/*`).
3. **`npm run test:e2e`** — the real, committed Playwright suite (`tests/e2e/*.spec.js`, added Phase 29; `npm run docs:stats` for current counts). This is the default way to verify a player-visible interaction — run it before reaching for a manual browser pass, not after. For a CSS-only change, run `npm run test:e2e -- visual-regression` specifically: it screenshots every gameplay screen at 1366×768 against the committed win32 baselines and will show you a pixel diff if anything moved. **It is not a check on cast art** — every screenshot containing a character hides `.hub-npc`/`[data-npc]` first, because NPCs animate on a rAF loop and are never pixel-stable, so a new or re-drawn sprite moves nothing here and must be verified by `character-sheet-geometry.test.js` and by eye. **Review a changed baseline before accepting it** — open the `-diff.png` and confirm every difference is one you intended; a blind `--update-snapshots` turns the whole suite into a rubber stamp.
4. **Full `npm run test` / `npm run lint` / `npm run build`** — at milestone boundaries or when the change touches shared infrastructure (a screen-routing guard, a repository facade, a quest-type contract all four types implement).
   **Teacher surfaces are reachable from e2e now** (Phase 144, `0143`). They had no coverage of any kind, structurally: the only way in is `dev-fake-teacher`, which signs into the **live** Supabase project, and a dashboard load alone issues a `POST` to `student_world_profiles` — a suite must not write to the database a real classroom is using. `tests/e2e/helpers/supabase-stub.js` intercepts `**/*.supabase.co/**` and answers from fixtures, so **nothing leaves the machine** and everything past the sign-in is the real application. Fixtures are the emptiest thing that gets through the door — one teacher, one classroom, `[]` for every other table — and a spec that needs rows names them. Get three PostgREST details right or the client throws in a way that looks nothing like its cause: `.single()` wants an object not an array (`Accept: …vnd.pgrst.object+json`), a write with `Prefer: return=representation` wants its own body back, and a count wants `content-range`. The helper returns its `writes` list so a spec can assert it stayed empty. **Note the route to an authoring form is four screens deep and Case 1.01 never reaches one** — a Map Mission is locked by design — and the wizard is name → preview → edit. **And the fixtures a spec names are where the defects are** (Phase 145, `0144`): the stub's defaults are an empty classroom, and an empty classroom has no submissions, so it has no Readiness column to be wrong. `teacher-classroom-with-students.spec.js` supplies the other end — a roster in all three `roster_slots.status` states with **two** claimed students, a world profile with progress, an overdue assignment, a submission carrying a real SAQ evaluation blob, and a manual grade against it — and found the Phase 145 defect on its first run. Two fixture details are worth copying: **a due date is an instant, not a date** (it is stored as end-of-day **local** via `new Date(input + "T23:59:59").toISOString()`, so a UTC-midnight fixture reads a day early in every zone behind Greenwich and looks exactly like an app defect — pin `timezoneId` and assert the report's counts and its overdue chip rather than a date string), and **the overdue branch needs two claimed students**, because it requires work still outstanding. **The assertion on these screens is "nothing threw", and it has to be asked for**: `render()` catches every renderer exception and swaps in the generic "Archive display recovered from a render issue" screen, so a `TypeError` on a teacher surface reaches nobody — watch the console for the `Chronicle render recovery` line the catch logs. A corollary: **a bare `toBeVisible()` in a caught world names the symptom and hides the cause** — when the render throws the rows simply never appear, and the wait reports a missing button five seconds after the `TypeError` that removed it. Poll for all three outcomes. And **going one level in is load-bearing on the Sources tab** — all three of `teacherUiState`'s `Set` fields are read from `sourceRowMarkup()`/`sourcePoolPreviewMarkup()`, neither of which runs until a unit section is expanded _and its pool has resolved_, so a sweep that only clicks the tab walks past the exact lines Phase 133's bug broke. **And a teacher's write is read back, so the stub's tables are live** (Phase 147, `0146`). Everything Phases 144 and 145 walk is a _read_, and both specs say so by asserting `supabase.writes` stayed empty — an honest assertion and the ceiling of a stub that answered reads from a frozen fixture and writes with an echo. It is a ceiling because **every consequential thing a teacher does is a write whose result they immediately read back**: `recordManualGrade()` re-reads the submission, `createCustomContent()` inserts **without an `id`** and the caller uses `row.id`, and `publishCaseSelections()` reads the **draft** row back out to publish it — so against an empty table, publishing took the revert branch and silently did nothing. An insert keeps its row now, a `PATCH` merges, a `DELETE` removes, an upsert honours its `on_conflict` target, and **an inserted row gets `id`/`created_at`/`updated_at` because the real table does** — `new Date(undefined)` renders as "Invalid Date" on the grading screen and looks exactly like a defect in the game. **Filters are parsed rather than ignored**, and an operator the stub does not understand is collected into `unsupportedFilters` rather than skipped, because silently widening a query is worse than saying so; every test asserts that list is empty, and the filtering itself is proved by a fixture grade belonging to a different evaluation that must never appear. The write walk (`tests/e2e/teacher-writes.spec.js`) found one defect on its first pass: **`editingCustomId` is seeded when the editor opens and never written again**, so the id of a row a save had just created was dropped and `persistAuthoringSelection()`'s update branch was unreachable within a session — three presses of Save Draft, three `custom_content_items` rows, each a whole authored question, none of them reachable or deletable from any screen. Nothing a student or teacher sees was wrong, which is why it survived. Fixed by recording the created row's id **and a new `editingCustomKind`**, not by moving `currentSlotKindAtStart` forward — `pick-question-type` reads that to decide whether re-picking a type may prefill from the official content, so reusing it would prefill an MCQ form from an evidence-organizing document. **And `/api/roster/*` is a second door, built in Phase 148** (`0147`) as `tests/e2e/helpers/roster-api-stub.js`. Provisioning, claiming, resolving a returning student's login and reissuing a password are the repo's **own serverless functions**, not Supabase, and under `npm run dev` those paths do not exist — so none of it was reachable, **including the student join flow, which is the first screen a real student ever meets**. What is under test is the client half; the stub owes those four endpoints their **contract** and their **rules**, because the rules are what make the client's failure branches reachable at all — a seat that is already claimed has to be refused or "Invalid join code or student ID." is a string no test can see. Two properties to preserve if you touch it: **claim and resolve-email answer every mismatch identically** so neither a join code nor a student ID can be enumerated, and **the Supabase stub checks the password of an account it created and leaves everything else permissive** — that way round because it mints the identity at `claim` and does not know the fixed `dev-fake-teacher` credentials, which must keep getting through. **And `POST /api/evaluate` is the third door, built in Phase 150** (`0149`) as `tests/e2e/helpers/evaluate-api-stub.js` — the only one a **student** talks to. Every written response in the game goes through it, `runEvaluation()` writes the `submissions` row **only on success**, and the path does not exist under `npm run dev`, so no submission could be produced at all and the student→teacher leg was unwalkable. Its reply is **built out of `RUBRICS`** — the same schema `api/evaluate.js` hands the model — so the rows, the `met` values and the readiness verdicts cannot drift from the contract, and its four refusals are copied from the endpoint so the client's 429/502 branches are reachable; a `met` that does not vary across the rows would show one third of what a reply renders, so the stub **cycles the enum**. **The same phase made the stub resolve embeds** (`EMBEDS` in `supabase-stub.js`), because `select` had been ignored entirely: a spec could fake `profiles!inner(display_name)` only by writing the join into its own fixture, which a row the _application_ inserts has nobody to do for it — a real student's submission reached the dashboard as "Unknown student" with an empty Readiness. `!inner` is honoured rather than ignored, so **a submission whose author has no `profiles` row is not shown at all**, and `options.tables` **replaces** a table rather than appending to it, so a fixture naming `profiles` must restate the stub teacher's own row or the dashboard never opens. An unanswerable join lands in `unsupportedEmbeds`, the twin of `unsupportedFilters`, because a blank column is not an error.

5. **A manual browser pass (`npm run dev`, by hand or via an MCP browser tool)** — only for an interaction genuinely uncovered by any committed spec. When you do this, **bank it**: add the scenario as a new `tests/e2e/*.spec.js` file (or a case in an existing one) so the next session doesn't re-derive it by hand. A manual pass that isn't banked is cost paid twice for the same coverage.

### The Spine Review — the closed part-by-part playtest program

`docs/playtest/PLAYTHROUGH-LEDGER.md` is the single source of program state: an ordered walk of the **student** experience, intro through unit completion, in thirteen parts. Read the ledger before starting a review part or acting on a defect report about a player-facing screen — a finding may already be recorded and routed. Each part gets its own `part-NN-*.md` **when it starts**, never before; the ledger holds status and counts and never finding text. The protocol and the severity/category taxonomy live in the ledger, in one place, deliberately.

**The program is closed** — all thirteen parts, 2026-08-03 to 2026-08-23. Don't go looking for an open row; the ledger's status table is the authority, and it records what is still open as individual routed S3s rather than as unfinished parts. Two rules from it that bind any session touching the program: **an S3 finding must name a destination** (a part number, the content queue, or an ADR), and **a part's ledger row is updated in the same commit as its fix** — if the row isn't in the diff, the part isn't closed. The program fixes behaviour, never shape: no restructuring, no new systems, no new content. Teacher surfaces are deliberately out of scope.

`?warp=<name>` (dev only, `DEV_WARPS` in `main.js`) boots straight into a named save state so a review pass starts where it starts — `intro`, `hall`, `hub`, `table`, `field`, `mission`, `railhead`, `port`, `reveal`, `reconstruct`, `unitclose`. It is gated on `import.meta.env.DEV` and verified absent from production builds; adding a state is one line in that table. **`window.__chronicleNav` is the other dev-only surface, gated the same way** (Phase 94): it samples `isFieldBlocked`/`isHubBlocked` across the active grid so the e2e walker can breadth-first a route through the room's real collision instead of sliding along walls. A test helper may read the game's state; it must not restate the game's rules — see `docs/decision-log/0093-the-walker-was-blind.md`. **And the walker is bounded by progress, not by the clock** (Phase 119, `0118`): it carried a 20-second deadline, and the longest walk in the suite covers 41.7 tiles, which is 11.4 seconds of key-hold on an idle machine — 60% of the budget spent on the ground the room is designed to make you cover. The deadline was also reporting a wedge as slowness, which is the part that mattered: the probe samples on a **half-tile lattice** and the player does not, so under load a body can come to rest a quarter tile off the row the plan cleared, inside something solid, and re-planning from there returns the same route. A stalled burst now squares up on the other axis, progress is measured toward the waypoint per axis, and nothing counts milliseconds. **A burst's floor is 150ms, and it is a frame budget rather than a duration** (Phase 121): a burst that renders only one frame can move less than `PROGRESS_TILES`, which the loop reads as a stall, and 70ms was about one frame once Phase 121 put the whole map frame on screen and gave the compositor more to do. **A test's verdict must not depend on how fast the machine is** — the rule `frame-budget.spec.js` was written for in Phase 93 — and **a fixed wait standing in for a state is the same defect**: the wall-slide case's window opened 240ms after its keydown and it read at 260ms, so it measured the free diagonal and compared it against itself. Ask the page, and where the window is narrower than a round trip, ask it **from inside the page**. **There are three dev probes now, all gated identically and all verified absent from production builds**: `__chronicleNav` (collision, Phase 94), `__chronicleCast` (the surface's authored cast — extended to the Institute's rooms in Phase 120, so it answers for a hub room as well as a field one) and `__chronicleReach` (Phase 120, `0119`), which returns `nearestFieldInteraction()`'s own answer to "what would `E` open right now". The last exists because `walkTo` only promises to reach _its own target_, and a spec that wants one of the other things in that nearest-wins list had no way to ask what it was about to get — `suburb-interiors.spec.js` asserted a locked record's refusal from a position it never checked, with 0.6 of a tile between the record and the man beside it. **Each of these reads the game's answer; none of them restates its rule**, which is the line `0093` drew and the reason a probe is the right shape here rather than arithmetic in a spec. **And the number that says a body is off the plan's row is not the number that says a burst moved it** (Phase 128, `0127`): the square-up gate above read `> PROGRESS_TILES`, the 0.03 noise floor for _did that burst move the body_, and was thereby asked a second question — _is the body standing on the row the route was drawn along_ — which got the first one's answer. The two offsets differ by an order of magnitude. Richmond's counting room: the route west to the book-keeper runs along lattice row y = 10.0, where the player's foot box ends at 10.78 and Nathan Purcell's blocking box begins at 10.80 — **0.02 of a tile, one pixel**, because `npcFootBox()` puts a body's edge at `anchor + 0.2` and this game posts people on `.6` offsets. The leg into that row is declared arrived by `ARRIVE_TILES` (0.3, fifteen times the clearance), falls short under load, ends at y = 10.025, and five thousandths of a tile of overlap stops the body dead at x = 5.260 — three reproductions, the same coordinate every time. The escape existed and 0.025 < 0.03 refused it. It is `OFF_ROW_TILES` (**one pixel**, because positions are written in pixels) now, in an exported pure `burstAxis()`. **Do not verify a walker fix with the suite**: the window is 0.01 of a tile, a sixth of what one frame moves the body, so it reproduced three times in twenty-four runs and then not once in forty-two more of the _unfixed_ walker — `tests/unit/walker-square-up.test.js` asks the decision both incidents' measured numbers instead. And **the world is not what to fix**: eleven bodies across the eighteen field surfaces leave exactly this one pixel, every one of them posted at `.6`, and these rooms' aisles are one tile tall by design. **And a walk's return value is an instant, not a state** (Phase 128, `0127` §5): `walkTo` promises the target was in reach at the frame the game's own `.is-near` appeared, and `field-talk` re-asks `isNearFieldNpc()` at the click — correct of the game, since a control the player cannot reach must refuse. For a stationed body the promise still holds a round trip later; for one of the **fifteen `kind: "wander"` bodies** it need not. Measured on the Taíno child, who wanders a 1.2-tile disc: over twelve runs the player arrived between 0.33 and 1.44 tiles away — every walk correct — and the child then moved up to 0.62 of a tile before the press, so **one run in twelve arrived at 0.99, pressed at 1.60 and got "Move closer to interact with Taíno child."** instead of a bubble. That was `activity-engines.spec.js`'s intermittent failure and it was no property of the walk. **Use `openFieldNpc()` for a body that moves** — it makes the walk and the press one operation and closes the gap again if the press is refused, because only the game can say whether it landed. A/B on the retry alone, same window both arms: **2 failures in 24 without it, 0 with it.** A stationed body does not need it and converting those would be churn. See `docs/decision-log/0127-one-pixel-of-daylight.md`. **And the criterion is `kind !== "station"`, not `kind === "wander"`** (Phase 146, `0145`): `0127` §5 fixed the Taíno child and said "three call sites, all three the same child", which reads as though the world divides into that child and stationed people. It does not — **`kind: "route"` walks too, and further.** A wanderer drifts inside a 1.2-tile disc; the settlement carpenter walks a **7.52-tile leg** between his barn yard and his bench, the burgess 4.61, the Powhatan man 3.35 and the woman 2.69, and all four were walked to and then pressed. The carpenter was the one flaky test in Phase 145's full-suite run of 404 — **4 refusals in 12 pressing after the walk, 0 in 12 with the two as one operation**, both arms in one file and one window — and he reaches `.is-near` at up to **1.42 tiles against a reach of 1.45**, three hundredths of a tile of margin, which he walks out of in about twenty milliseconds. Guarded statically by `tests/unit/walked-bodies-do-not-walk-away.test.js`, which resolves every `walkToNpc()` id in the suite against the behaviour tables and fails on any that is not stationed. **Scope that lookup to the `*_BEHAVIOURS` tables** — a bare `liaison: {` at two spaces is also how `HUB_TARGETS` opens Emery Voss's hub marker, and a file-wide search reported her as not stationed on a surface she is stationed on all nine of. See `docs/decision-log/0145-a-body-that-moves-does-not-wait.md`.
