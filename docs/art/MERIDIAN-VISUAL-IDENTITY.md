# Meridian Visual Identity

How the rival institute looks, why it looks descended from Chronicle, and the asset specifications
that follow from the sprite contract this repo already ships.

Written in Phase 78 alongside decision log `0061`. **No art is generated by this document.** Phase 7
produces the insignia and concept sheets; Phase 8 the Liaison sprite; Phase 11 the revealed variant.
Read [`CHARACTER-SPRITESHEET-STANDARD.md`](./CHARACTER-SPRITESHEET-STANDARD.md) for the renderer and
[`CHARACTER-CAST-SPEC.md`](./CHARACTER-CAST-SPEC.md) for the generation parameters — this file adds
Meridian on top of both and contradicts neither.

---

## 1. The one idea

Chronicle communicates **preservation**: accumulation, institutional memory, warm archival space,
visible age and repair, evidence gathered across generations.

Meridian communicates **direction**: precision, deliberate correction, controlled movement, and a
belief that history can be steered somewhere better.

Chronicle asks _what does the evidence allow us to defend?_ Meridian asks _if we know where history
should go, why refuse to guide it?_ The art must carry that difference before the player can name
it.

Meridian is elegant, practical, historically conscious, and slightly ahead of Chronicle. **Not
futuristic** — nothing in their kit should look like it came from a later century than the game's
frame story.

## 2. Anchor glass is shared, and must not be recoloured

The load-bearing decision, and the one most likely to be got wrong by a well-meaning pass.

Meridian's technology **descended from Chronicle's**. The player should be able to see that. The
mechanism that makes it visible is the material: both institutions use anchor glass, and anchor
glass lights the same colour for both.

Chronicle already ships that colour. `--c-info: #7ee6ec` is a pale cyan the design system has used
since Phase 44. Phase 7 aliases it rather than inventing a second one:

```css
--c-glass: var(--c-info); /* #7ee6ec — anchor glass. SHARED. Never recoloured per faction. */
```

**Anchor glass is not a Meridian colour and never becomes one.** The difference between the two
institutes is the _housing_ around the glass, never the glass:

|         | Chronicle                               | Meridian                                 |
| ------- | --------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------- |
| Housing | Brass, bronze, wood, archival case work | Dark iron, oxidized copper, dark leather |
| Cut     | Circular, layered, set into a frame     | Thin geometric strips, directional       |
| Form    | Fixed, heavy, repaired                  | Folding, rotating, portable              |
| Reads   | Old and cared for                       | New, exact, made to be carried           |

Do not introduce a second fictional substance. Anchor glass is enough, and a second one would be a
new term to teach for no gain — `CHRONICLE-CANON.md` §8.

## 3. Palette

Restrained, and readable at pixel-art sizes. These are proposed token names for Phase 7; they extend
the `--c-*` channel convention in `global.css` rather than replacing anything.

```css
--c-meridian: #1f4a4e; /* dark teal — primary */
--c-meridian-deep: #12312f; /* blue-black — shadow */
--c-meridian-fabric: #4a7c6f; /* muted sea green — secondary fabric and equipment */
--c-meridian-copper: #9a6b46; /* oxidized / desaturated brass — hardware */
--c-meridian-ivory: #e8e2d2; /* documents, markings, interface contrast */
```

A caution the palette audit turned up: Chronicle already owns a **bright** teal
(`--c-teal-rgb: 126, 221, 214`) as an accent. Meridian's teal must stay clearly **darker and more
desaturated**, or the two read as the same institution on a small sprite. `#1f4a4e` against
`#7eddd6` is a comfortable separation; anything lighter than roughly `#2d6a6a` is not.

**Never use:** generic villain red-and-black, radioactive green, heavy purple, cyberpunk neon,
sterile white science-fiction rooms, or costumes resembling superheroes or powered armour.

## 4. Shapes, motifs, materials

**Shapes.** Long vertical lines. Compass and meridian-line motifs. Divided circles. Clean diagonals.
Narrow pointed forms. Equipment aligned to a grid rather than arranged by habit.

Chronicle stays circular, layered, warm and archival. Meridian is cleaner, directional, intentional.
Where Chronicle's props look like they accumulated, Meridian's look like they were specified.

**Materials.** Dark leather. Weathered teal fabric. Oxidized copper. Dark iron. Thin anchor-glass
strips. Folding measuring tools, mechanical compasses, map cases. Field notebooks narrower and more
orderly than Chronicle's. Equipment built to **alter or redirect** an imprint, where Chronicle's only
observes — that functional difference should be visible in the silhouette of the tool.

## 5. The insignia

Five deliverables for Phase 7, in this order. Each later one is derived from the first by
subtraction, not redrawn.

1. **Full insignia** — the complete mark, for documents, doors, and interface headers.
2. **Sprite-scale** — legible at roughly 12–16 px. This is the real constraint.
3. **One-colour** — a single ink, for stamps and printed records.
4. **Engraved** — the mark as it appears cut into metal equipment.
5. **Partial mark** — a fragment that reads as _a mark_ without identifying Meridian. This is the
   early-unit clue, and it must be recognisable in hindsight after the reveal.

**Do not finalise a symbol that fails below the game's normal object resolution.** Test at
sprite-scale first and work upward; a mark designed at full size and shrunk is how you get a smudge
on a 16-pixel map prop. A divided circle crossed by a single off-vertical line survives the
reduction; anything with more than three strokes does not.

## 6. The Field Liaison — character direction

Emery Voss. See [`../design/THE-FIELD-LIAISON.md`](../design/THE-FIELD-LIAISON.md) for who they are.

**Silhouette.** Lean and practical. A mid-length asymmetrical coat or field jacket, with one
shoulder carrying the stronger visual weight. A compact cross-body notebook or instrument case. High
boots that suit several historical settings. A narrow anchor-glass instrument at the belt or chest.
One small teal accent visible from **every** direction. Hair and head shape clearly distinct from
the Director and from both player appearances.

The asymmetry is doing the work: it is what makes the character identifiable at 45 pixels of body
from any facing.

**Avoid** long robes (they destroy a walk cycle), oversized props, capes, modern tactical gear, and
small accessories that vanish at sprite scale. Avoid any full Meridian branding in the early units.

### Two states, two sheet keys

**Chronicle-facing** (`liaison`) — mostly compatible with Chronicle field clothing. Meridian colours
appear only as accents. The instrument is visually ambiguous. No insignia. Warm-neutral outer layer,
cool teal details.

**Revealed** (`liaison-meridian`) — the coat opened, reversed, or partly changed to expose the dark
teal lining. Insignia visible. Anchor-glass instrument active, pale cyan used sparingly. **Same
silhouette, same face, same core clothing.**

The reveal must read as _details that were already there_, not a costume swap. Practically: design
the revealed version first, then cover it up to produce the Chronicle-facing one. Doing it the other
way round produces two characters who happen to share a haircut.

### PixelLab generation spec

Bound by the shipped contract. Every value below is taken from the real pipeline, not assumed.

| Parameter      | Value                                                                                                |
| -------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| `size`         | **40** — this is a body-height argument, _not_ the canvas. 88 is wrong; see `CHARACTER-CAST-SPEC.md` |
| `view`         | `low top-down`                                                                                       |
| `outline`      | `single color black outline`                                                                         |
| `shading`      | `detailed shading`                                                                                   |
| `detail`       | `high detail`                                                                                        |
| `proportions`  | `{"type": "preset", "name": "heroic"}`                                                               |
| Rotations      | 8 returned; the game uses 4                                                                          |
| Walk animation | `walking-8-frames` **template — not `v3`**                                                           |

**Why the template and not `v3`.** v3 draws the back view receding: the body climbs 4–6 px up the
canvas across the north cycle and snaps back at the loop point, which reads in game as a limp. The
template also fixes the frame count at 8.

**Canvas.** The build packs to the pinned **48×56** canvas, feet on row 49, standing body 45 rows.
`canonicalCanvas()` clamps rather than resizing the cast, so a long prop on a new character is
**clipped, not accommodated** — which is exactly why the brief above forbids oversized props. Strips
are `[standing, walk0 … walk7]`; `STAND_COLUMN = 1`; direction keys `down`/`up`/`left`/`right`
mapping to `south`/`north`/`west`/`east`.

**Deliverables:** idle and walk in all four directions, for both states — 8 strips per state — plus
the portrait the build already produces for every character. Portraits are single stills;
**there is no expression-swap system**, so do not order an expression set until one exists.

**Palette relationship.** Generate against the existing cast, not in isolation. Director Hale is the
style anchor and **must not be regenerated**. Voss must read as the same art style, the same weight
of outline, and the same light direction as the 28 characters already shipped.

**Cost.** Roughly 1 generation for the create, plus 1 per direction for a template walk. Two states
is therefore about 10 generations. The account's subscription allowance is exhausted and billing
falls back to credit at roughly $0.02 per job.

**Do not generate until the character direction is approved.** That is a standing rule from the
brief, and it is also just economics.

## 7. Meridian locations

Meridian does not get an evil headquarters. Its spaces should show an organisation that began with a
purpose and drifted from it.

1. A **mobile field station** concealed inside a historical location.
2. A small **observation chamber** built from modified Chronicle equipment.
3. An orderly **evidence room** holding records Chronicle refused to preserve.
4. A later **client-facing planning room** — more polished, and more compromised.
5. A final **divided headquarters**, humanitarian operatives on one side of it and client-funded
   leadership on the other.

The visual arc across those five: improvised → confident → wealthy → divided → exposed.

**Build the modular language first, not the buildings.** Phase 7 produces materials, props and
motifs that later spaces reuse. Building five environments before the language is settled would mean
five sets of one-off art, which is the mistake the canonical tile palette exists to prevent.
