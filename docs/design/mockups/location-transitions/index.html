When it fires

  • A warp / Chronotravel to a field map.
  • Entering a new building — the Institute, a settlement's hall.
  • Not room-to-room inside one building — those stay seamless.

What's on screen

  • A stylized illustration of the destination — a real depicted place, not just geometry.
  • A circular “Syncing” loader that fills — the record catching up to where you are.
  • Subtle motion — the scene gently breathes, light drifts, the water shimmers, window-light flickers. It fades softly into the navy rather than sitting as a hard photo.
  • Light geometric framing (corner ticks, a rotating tick-ring) layered over the art.
  • For a case: its name, date, and goal — the Atlantic Crossroads card, kept.

Where the art comes from

These SVG scenes are stand-ins. A painterly image (like the reference paintings) drops straight into this same frame — the fade and motion layers sit on top of whatever the backdrop is. Options, cheapest first:

  • A painterly key illustration per location — the reference-art look.
  • A stylized render of the real map (you already have the tiles).
  • One reusable frame + per-place art — the layout never changes.
