# 0037 — The Institute Main Hall becomes a real tiled room

Status: accepted · Phase 54 · 2026-07-29

## Context

Every playable surface in Chronicle was built from the tile library except one: the Chronicle
Institute's Main Hall. It was a single hand-drawn top-down floor plan,
`apps/web/src/assets/institute/chronicle-institute-hub.png`, stretched with `object-fit: cover` to
fill its box, with the three staff NPCs and the three interactive objects positioned over it as
percentages of that box.

That had four consequences worth naming, because each one is a class of defect rather than a
cosmetic complaint:

1. **It read as a diagram, not a room.** One door away, the Archive Room is a real `.tmj` with
   furniture, lighting, and a camera (decision log 0030). Walking between the two was a visible
   style break — the Main Hall's "bookshelves" were flat coloured rectangles.
2. **Collision was transcribed by hand.** `HUB_BLOCK_RECTS` was eight rects hand-measured against a
   bitmap, with no mechanism to keep them true. Phase 53 removed exactly this pattern from the three
   field maps in favour of collision generated from the stamps that painted the art
   (decision log 0036); the Main Hall was the last place it survived.
3. **Nothing could have depth.** A painted background cannot put the player behind a shelf or a
   potted plant, because there is no layer above them.
4. **Percentage positioning was load-bearing and fragile.** Two `!important` percentage overrides on
   `.hub-trophy` (`top: 11.5%`, `top: 12.8%`) existed only to nudge the Preservation Case button onto
   a shelf drawn near the top-left of that one PNG.

## Decision

Rebuild the Main Hall as `apps/web/src/content/maps/institute-hall.tmj`, generated by
`scripts/generate-institute-hall-tmj.js` on top of `scripts/lib/map-builder.js` — the same pipeline
the three field maps use — and give `HUB_GRID` a `tile` so it becomes a camera room like the Archive
Room. Collision comes from the generated `institute-hall.blocks.js`. The PNG is deleted.

### Sheet family: Medieval Tavern, plus two narrow additions

The palette is the same three Medieval Tavern sheets the Archive Room and the onboarding hallway
already glob, so the three Institute interiors read as one building and Vite bundles no additional
art for those three. Two additions:

- **`Medieval Tavern/Auto-tile-A4-walls-2.png`** — the hall has real walls, which the Archive Room
  does not (there the perimeter is implied by furniture runs). Only the flat, full-bleed surface rows
  are used. The A4 corner/edge blob set is deliberately untouched: a full-bleed material paves an
  arbitrary shape correctly on its own, which is the same property that lets Philadelphia's cobble
  pave an irregular plaza.

  This corrects a stale claim in `scripts/generate-hallway-tmj.js`, which said these sheets are "an
  RPG-Maker-style autotile blob layout `tiled-map-loader.js` can't parse." The loader parses them
  fine; what it cannot do is _choose_ a blob cell. Restricting the palette to the surface rows
  sidesteps that entirely, and this map proves it.

- **`Island survival/5.png`** — exactly three Institute artifacts, all with transparent surrounds:
  the compass-rose Navigation Table, a brass mariner's compass, and the plinth the Preservation Case
  displays on. Nothing in the Medieval Tavern family reads as navigation or as a museum case; the
  nearest candidates are a tavern bar and a wine rack. This is the same narrow prop-only borrow the
  Caribbean palette already makes for Medieval harbor's ship hulls, and the chart table on this very
  sheet is already live on the Caribbean map.

**Rejected:** the `office/3` + `office/4` + `19th Century European City/tile-B-04` restyle that
`archive-room.palette.js`'s header records as a candidate. It would give the Institute a
period-appropriate scholarly interior, but applying it to one of the two Institute rooms would leave
the Main Hall and the Archive Room in different visual languages. That is a whole-Institute restyle
with its own sign-off, not a side effect of this rebuild.

### Size: 23x12, chosen for the height

23x12 at 48px is 1104x576 against a rendered `.institute-map` frame of roughly 970x596 at 1366x768.

The height is the load-bearing half. 576 < 596, so `updateHubCamera()` never scrolls vertically and
the north wall band — which carries the Institute's gold and navy pennants — is always fully in
frame. A first pass at 23x14 scrolled 76px and sliced every banner in half at the top of the
viewport: precisely the cut-off-art defect Phase 53 was about. Horizontal scroll is kept and wanted;
1104 > 970, so the hall reads as wider than the window and the camera pans east and west.

The three targets the guided tour highlights (Preservation Case, Archive Room door, Navigation Table)
are all inside the spawn view, which matters because the tour locks movement while pulsing them.

### Layout is four bands, and the dais approach is deliberate

Rows 2-3 are record stacks along the north wall with cols 11-13 left clear as the Archive Room
approach; rows 4-5 an open cross-aisle; rows 6-7 two transcription tables with a one-tile lane
between them at col 10; rows 8-9 the south band with cols 10-13 left clear as the foyer runner.

The whole south side of the Navigation Table dais stays clear so reaching it is a straight walk east
from the foyer and one step north. An earlier revision put the brass compass in that corridor and
turned reaching the hub's single most important object into a two-detour walk — caught by writing the
e2e spec as two hold-until-blocked moves rather than timed presses.

## Consequences

- `hubPointStyle()` lost its percentage branch. Both hub rooms declare a `tile`, and keeping the
  branch would have left a trap where a new room that forgot to declare one silently laid itself out
  in percentages against a box of a different shape.
- `updateInstituteNpcs()` had two more copies of the same percentage math, written on every 120ms
  tick. They overwrote correct markup on the very next frame after render; both now call
  `hubPointStyle()`.
- `.hub-npc`'s `width: 7.1%` is now overridden inside `.hub-world` by a fixed pixel width matched to
  the player, so an NPC no longer changes size when a room's grid width changes.
- `.hallway-door` was a cropped slice of the deleted PNG, positioned by magic numbers
  (`background-size: 650%`, `background-position: 29% 92%`) that only meant anything relative to that
  one bitmap. It is now drawn with CSS gradients in the project's navy/gold/timber palette: the Main
  Hall's panelled south wall with light spilling through its open doorway.
- `tests/e2e/hub-movement.spec.js` was rewritten. Its `hubTileFromStyle()` inverted percentage
  positioning and would have broken by construction; it now reads pixels, asserts against two named
  generated wall rects, and adds a second case walking to the Navigation Table and opening it.
- `canonical-palette.js` gained `wall.interior.plank`, `wall.interior.stone`,
  `floor.institute.wood` and `floor.institute.sandstone`. It also now records that
  `floor.archive.sandstone` is a **misnomer** — that entry's art is plank wood, and using it for the
  alcove would have painted the alcove and the dais in the same material. The key is kept because
  `archive-room.palette.js` references it and renaming would churn a live map for nothing.
- `npm run maps:build` now exists and regenerates all six maps. Every generator is reproducible:
  running it against the committed tree produces no diff outside the new map.

## Not done, deliberately

`scripts/generate-archive-room-tmj.js` still uses its own local `stamp()` rather than `MapBuilder`,
and `ARCHIVE_ROOM_BLOCK_RECTS` is still hand-measured. Migrating it would be right, and would also
fix three over-declared footprints in `archive-room.palette.js` that this pass found while measuring
the same sheets (`recordShelf` and `recordCanisters` are stamped taller than their art, so they draw
a slice of the unit below). That is a second room's regression surface and belongs in its own pass.
