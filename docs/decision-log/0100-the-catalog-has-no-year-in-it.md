# 0100 — The catalog has no year in it

**Phase 101 · 2026-08-31 · Accepted**

Unit 9's art reconnaissance: the campus quadrangle, the reading room and the processing room beneath
it, surveyed against the pixels. Two gaps registered, one exclusion rule established, one candidate
gap deliberately refused, and **nothing commissioned** — see §6.

Second slice of Unit 9, after Phase 100 authored its content (`0099`). Follows `0095`, which is the
equivalent slice for Unit 8 and whose method this phase reuses without variation.

---

## 1. The survey said yes, and that goes first

Three phases running have opened with a register entry that could not close. This one did not, and
reporting that plainly is part of the job: **a reconnaissance that only ever comes back with gaps
stops being believed, and then the next author skips it.**

`p8-campus` has sat in the forward slate since Phase 81 with no `gap` field at all, and the honest
result of checking it is that the entry was right. `University/tile-B-04` is the strongest single
exterior sheet in the library for an American state university — brick and concrete blocks of the
1960s and 70s drawn at map scale, a porticoed administration building, running tracks, bike
shelters. The reading room's tables, chairs, double-sided stacks, panelled walls and binder shelves
are all in `office/3` and `office/4`. The processing room's work counter is `office/2`'s
cabinet-and-counter run; its filing cabinets and tall double-door storage cupboards are `office/1`'s.

**The map is not blocked.** That is a different finding from Unit 8's, where a subdivision had no
houses in it, and it should be recorded as a different finding rather than dressed up as a narrow
escape.

## 2. The catalog has no year in it, and that is what the survey was actually for

The one question the existing documentation could not answer turned out to be the only one that
mattered, and it is a question about **when**, not about what.

`TILE-LIBRARY-CATALOG.md` describes `office/1` as "desks with computers" and `office/3` as "desks
with open books and papers". Both are accurate. Neither is usable, because the sheets disagree
about the decade:

- **`office/1`'s four workstations are beige CRTs** — deep chassis, thick bezel, a vent grille under
  a screen much smaller than the box holding it. Correct for 1998, and the only such computer in 250
  sheets.
- **`office/2`'s and `office/3`'s are flat panels.** 2005 at the earliest.

A reading room furnished out of all three sheets — which is what the catalog invites, since it
recommends `office/3` in bold as "the single best Institute Archive fit in the library" — puts seven
years on one desk and twenty across the room, in a unit whose entire argument is that the date of a
copy decides what it says.

**The catalog's schema has no time axis.** Its columns are sheet, contents, verdict, periods, and
the `periods` column records where a sheet _may_ be used rather than what decade the objects on it
depict — `office` is marked `9` throughout, which is true of all three sheets and distinguishes none
of them. Every map from here on is set in a year. So the rule this phase adds is narrow and
mechanical rather than clever: **when a map's period turns on an object the catalog describes
generically, open the sheet.** The prose is a finding aid, not the record — which is, without
anybody planning it, the thing Unit 9's own content is about.

The correction is in the catalog now, on `office/1`'s row, and the exclusion is in both new planned
maps: **every screen on this map comes from `office/1`.** That is an exclusion list, not a gap — the
same shape as `p8-suburb`'s Highway Rest Area rule, and the second phase running where the real
discipline of a survey was what to leave out.

## 3. Two objects are absent, and both are named by records the player is sent to read

Registered in `canonical-palette.js` and mirrored into the style guide's table:

**`prop.archive.recordCarton`** — the grey flip-top document box: singly, stacked, and in a shelved
run, with manila folders standing in it. `campus-two-editions` cites BOX 3, FOLDER 11, and the
processing room's whole job is boxing, foldering and describing. The library's storage furniture is
filing cabinets and bookcases, and **the filing cabinet is not a near miss — it is the wrong
object.** A drawer of hanging files says _current business records_, which is precisely what an
archive is not. This is what makes a room read as an archive rather than as an office, which is a
sentence the register was already carrying one entry down for `prop.meridian.chartCase`.

**`prop.archive.microfilmReader`** — beige console, a large screen raked back about thirty degrees, a
film carriage beneath it, two spool arms. This is the sharper of the two. `campus-two-editions` names
REEL 47 and the target frame preceding it, and the mission it feeds is the chain paper → film → scan;
**without a reader the middle step of that chain has no object anywhere in the room the mission is
set in.** Partial rather than absent: `University/tile-B-05`'s AV lecterns at (4,4) and (4,10) are
grey consoles with raked screens and pass at map scale. Neither has reels, and the reels are the
entire difference between a microfilm reader and a control panel.

Both entries name a thing that could arrive in a parcel, which is `0095` §1's test, and both describe
the geometry rather than the object, which is `0095` §5's — third phase running.

## 4. One candidate was refused, and refusing it is the point of having a register

A **modern card catalogue** — the oak cabinet of small drawers with brass pulls — is absent from the
library in any idiom later than `Steampunk/5`'s Victorian brass one. It is the most obvious thing in
a picture of a 1998 library, and it is **not registered**, because no record in the unit names one:
the finding aid is a bound thirty-page guide on a table, not a drawer of cards.

A **1990s automobile** is absent for the same reason it was absent in Phase 96 — `Highway Rest Area`'s
forty vehicles are contemporary. Also not registered: the map is a quadrangle, no record names a car,
and seventeen years late at map scale is not the fifty that made Unit 8 commission a fleet.

The register's value is entirely in what it refuses. An entry added because a room in a photograph
would have one is how a shopping list becomes a mood board, and the two entries in §3 are defensible
precisely because these two were turned down in the same pass.

## 5. October came free, and the catalog was right about that

`Modern Park/tile-B-05` carries five autumn crowns and three of them have fallen leaves drawn at the
base, which is the detail that makes a month read as a month rather than as a colour choice. The
catalog had already recorded this, in the words "summer and autumn dress", and nobody had checked.

Worth noting beside §2 rather than buried: the same document was right in the place it was expected
to be thin and useless in the place it was expected to be sufficient. That is an argument for
checking, not an argument against the catalog.

## 6. Nothing was commissioned, and the reason is money rather than judgement

The two gaps in §3 are real, and under `0095`'s precedent this phase would have generated them into a
`derived/campus-archive.png`. It did not. **The PixelLab balance is $16.76 with the subscription's
generations exhausted**, and Phase 96's own record is that six accepted objects cost three rounds —
every early car came back a show car, and two house rolls came back at a scale that was invisible in
the preview. A two-object commission at that hit rate is a material fraction of what is left, and
spending it is a decision for the repository's owner rather than a step in a build order.

So the durable half shipped and the purchase did not. That is not a deferral of the work: the survey,
the two register entries, the exclusion rule and the two planned-map entries are what a future
session actually reads, and they are what stop the _next_ author re-opening 250 sheets. The map can
also be built without either object — a reading room dressed from `office/3` and `office/4` with the
AV lectern standing in for the reader is a real room, flagged, in the way `p7-depression-street`'s
commercial block is flagged.

## 7. What was deliberately not done

- **No map, no interiors, no cast, no activities**, and `main.js` is untouched. Unit 9 is still
  absent from `UNITS` and still lands a player on the wrong continent if registered early — `0075`.
  The map is the next slice.
- **No commission**, per §6.
- **No new pack purchase proposed.** Both gaps are single props, and the `derived-objects` pipeline
  exists for exactly this.
- **`p8-campus` keeps its id and its period.** It is Period 8's entry, its sheets serve Period 9
  unchanged, and renaming it to match the unit that first builds on it would be the `p8-suburb`
  rename of Phase 81 run backwards for no gain. What it gained is the confirmation, in its notes.
- **The reading room and the processing room are two entries, not one.** They differ in their
  sheets, their gap and their light, and a "campus archive interior" entry covering both would be
  `architecture.indigenous.northAmerican` again — a category, filed as a job.

it: the whole method is looking at pixels and asking what year they are.
